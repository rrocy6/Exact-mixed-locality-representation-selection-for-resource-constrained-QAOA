\paragraph{D01: raw diagnostic versus prepared-state excess.}
The selector guardrail is evaluated on the archived, unclipped relaxation
moments with threshold $\tau=0.02$ and numerical tolerance $10^{-9}$.
Applying warm-start clipping with $\delta=0.05$ to those same moment vectors
produces threshold exceedances in 5 of 60 oracle, 43 of 180 compilation and
8 of 72 QAOA-pool selected designs. These diagnostics do not identify the
selector LP representative with the first-optimum representative used by
original E4. Prepared-state excess is reported separately; the raw selector
guardrail is not claimed to bound it.

\paragraph{D02: repaired selector and historical experiment identities.}
The repaired beam retains prefixes with infeasible native completions, computes
Pareto ranks separately by feasibility on the entire candidate level, skips
zero-range crowding coordinates, uses the ceiling half-score allocation, and
retains the best feasible design across all completed candidate evaluations.
Beam truncation remains heuristic. Re-evaluation matches the oracle optimum on
58 of 60 instances, with maximum score regret $0.01015625$; the mean regret is
$2.3003472222\times10^{-4}$. Two oracle action vectors change. Of the 960
oracle sensitivity settings, six action vectors differ from their archived
counterparts. The repaired selector changes 65 of 180 compilation designs,
10 of 72 QAOA-pool designs and seven of 20 fixed targeted warm-start designs.
The latter comparison reuses archived moments and does not re-screen instances.
The resource and QAOA observations remain attached to their historical designs.
Results for changed representations require the separately listed recompilation
and optimisation tasks before they can be attributed to the repaired selector.

\paragraph{D03: operational certificate scope.}
The certifier permits an initially absent incumbent, represented by
$U_J=+\infty$, and returns the committed frontier and its lower bound when a
node, evaluation, time or explicit interruption limit is reached. An infeasible
native representation does not terminate the search. Feasibility uses the
floating-point raw-moment diagnostic with declared tolerance $10^{-9}$;
the returned bounds certify this implemented feasible set under the fixed
reference compiler. They are not exact-arithmetic certificates for an
unrounded LP feasible set. The previously completed 60 certification traces
remain historical records and are not replaced by interrupted runs.

\paragraph{D04--D05: resource aggregation.}
For each representation draw, first take the median over the five transpiler
seeds; then average the five matched-random draw medians within each instance.
Form paired differences afterwards and report their median across instances.
For Max-3SAT, the all-to-all random-minus-native depth median is $-16$.
On the 12-instance common-fit Max-3SAT grid subset, the random-minus-native
medians are $+6.5$ gates, $-3.9$ depth, $+1.8$ SWAPs and $+4.8$ routing units.
These correct the aggregation of frozen designs; they do not describe a new
compilation run for repaired-selector designs.

\paragraph{D06: coefficient range.}
Compute the coefficient range after collecting Pauli terms, using only
nonzero, non-identity coefficients. The frozen compilation-tier selective
medians are $4.5$ for cubic spin glass and $57.5$ for Max-3SAT. The
added-topology export now uses the same Pauli coefficient-range definition,
rather than a maximum polynomial coefficient.

\paragraph{D07: corrected raw archive.}
The complete original correction ZIP has been checked against its manifest;
4,800 task identities comprise 4,332 completed optimisation reruns and 468
validated reused rows. Restart, draw and instance aggregation reproduces the
60 primary corrected warm-start contrasts. Publication of the original ZIP
must be documented by its actual release receipt, exact commit, download URL
and SHA-256; no public-availability statement is inferred from a local audit.

\paragraph{D08: realised E6 depth and instance-level statistics.}
For each representation draw, the initial E6 depth is reconstructed as
$\lfloor B/g_\sigma\rfloor$ from the archived budget and one-layer gate count.
The archived realised depth is used after checking the full-circuit budget,
parameter-vector dimension, and equality of depth, compiler seed and optimised
parameters across noise levels. Of 144 representation-draw groups, 44 have a
realised depth one smaller than the initial estimate. Three restarts and then
five random draws are averaged within each raw instance. E3 and E6 uncertainty
uses two-sided Student-$t$ intervals with $n-1$ degrees of freedom across
instances. These depth checks reconstruct provenance from archived rows;
they do not repeat every historical full-circuit transpilation.

\paragraph{E5: retained correction from the first closeout.}
There are 272 scheduled endpoints, of which 191 are estimable: 53 help,
121 little effect and 17 hurt; 81 are expected sparse-device infeasibilities.
The residual ``little effect'' class includes uncertain intervals: 38 of its
121 intervals extend outside the declared tolerance band. It must not be
read as evidence of equivalence or uniformly small effects. The QAOA effect
uses $(\mathrm{random}-\mathrm{selective})/\max(1,|f^\star|)$ and tolerance
$0.01$; the compilation endpoints use the paired control resource denominator
and tolerance $0.05$, as recorded by the E5 reconstruction entrypoint.
