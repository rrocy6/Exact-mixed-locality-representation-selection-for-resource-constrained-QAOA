\title{Exact Selective Locality Reduction for QAOA: From Sharp Penalties to Certified Representation Selection}

\author{Tom Liu}
\affiliation{Department of Mathematics, University of Warwick, Coventry CV4 7AL, United Kingdom}
\email{Tom.Liu@warwick.ac.uk}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Higher-order pseudo-Boolean objectives offer compact formulations for combinatorial optimisation, but their use in QAOA creates a trade-off between interaction locality, auxiliary qubits, penalty couplings, and compiled circuit cost. We develop an exact selective quadratization framework in which only chosen higher-order terms are reduced. For arbitrary-degree objectives, we derive a sharp aggregate-residual penalty and prove that successive substitutions preserve pointwise exactness. For cubic objectives, this yields a closed-form mixed-sign threshold for auxiliaries shared across multiple terms.

We then formulate compiler-aware representation selection over this exact design space using resource metrics and relaxation-derived fibre-risk constraints. A finite anytime search returns feasible representations and can additionally provide an optimality-gap certificate. The same relaxation defines a consistency-aware warm start for both original and auxiliary qubits. We compare native, fully quadratized, selective, and matched-random representations on Max-3SAT and cubic spin-glass instances under common computational budgets, enabling a controlled study of when selective quadratization is advantageous.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A pseudo-Boolean optimisation problem has the form
\begin{equation}
    \min_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}} f(x),
    \qquad
    f(x)=\sum_{A\subseteq[n]} c_A x_A,
    \qquad
    x_A:=\prod_{i\in A}x_i .
    \label{eq:intro-pbo}
\end{equation}
The degree of $f$ is the largest interaction order in its unique multilinear
representation.  Higher-order terms occur naturally in clause penalties,
hypergraph objectives, and many-body spin models, and they can encode such
structure with substantially fewer variables or couplings than an imposed
quadratic formulation~\cite{BorosHammer2002,Hadfield2021,Glos2022,CampbellDahl2022}.

For the Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm (QAOA)~\cite{Farhi2014},
the representation is not merely an algebraic preprocessing choice.  Under the
standard diagonal map
\[
    x_i\longmapsto N_i:=\frac{I-Z_i}{2},
\]
a degree-$k$ monomial becomes a diagonal interaction of locality at most $k$.
The original QAOA construction therefore permits higher-order cost terms
without first converting them to QUBO form.  Problem-specific studies have used
this freedom for graph colouring and space-efficient binary
encodings~\cite{CampbellDahl2022,Glos2022}; higher-order QAOA has also been
analysed on random hypergraphs and spin-glass models~\cite{Basso2022} and
implemented for hardware-tailored cubic Ising instances in short-depth,
whole-chip circuits~\cite{PelofskeShortDepth2024,Pelofske2024}.
Endpoint comparisons on continuous-optimisation benchmarks and routing
formulations found that higher-order encodings can use fewer qubits, while the
decomposition of their multi-qubit phases into one- and two-qubit gates can
increase circuit cost~\cite{Stein2023,Bell2025}.  Even when locality is
kept fixed, the ordering and synthesis of higher-order phase gadgets can
materially change logical depth~\cite{Verchere2023}.  More generally,
limited connectivity can make routing a leading contribution to QAOA
depth~\cite{Weidenfeller2022}.  These results expose a problem- and
hardware-dependent width--depth trade-off, not a uniformly preferable
locality.  A recent problem-specific study likewise reports substantial
resource savings from higher-order encodings on several selected optimisation
tasks~\cite{Koch2026}, reinforcing that the comparison depends on the
encoding as well as the polynomial degree.  Related endpoint trade-offs also
arise in quantum annealing: direct
PUBO encodings of 3-SAT can avoid auxiliary variables and change the spectral
gap and coefficient-scale requirements, although those results concern
annealing rather than QAOA~\cite{Nagies2025}.

The standard route to lower locality is \emph{quadratization}.  Products are
replaced by auxiliary binary variables and quadratic penalties enforcing the
intended product relations.  Rosenberg's pair substitution is the canonical
example~\cite{Rosenberg1975}; subsequent work established pointwise
quadratization as the standard notion of exactness and developed term-wise,
sign-specific, aggregative, and compact constructions with different
auxiliary-variable and coefficient requirements~\cite{FreedmanDrineas2005,
Ishikawa2011,BorosGruber2014,AnthonyEtAl2017,
BorosCramaRodriguezHeck2020,Dattani2019}.  Optimisation-based schemes have also
selected substitutions so as to reduce the number of auxiliaries and the
penalty magnitude~\cite{VermaLewis2020}; application-specific QUBO
reformulations similarly expose coupling and physical-qubit overheads for
Max-3SAT~\cite{Nusslein2023}.  In particular, Verma, Lewis, and
Kochenberger explicitly reuse one pair auxiliary across several cubic terms
and propose an aggregate penalty calibration~\cite{VermaLewisKochenberger2021}.
A recent unifying treatment organises complete quadratization into recursive
monomial decomposition, auxiliary enforcement, and subsequent
convexification~\cite{CramaEtAl2025}.  Thus shared auxiliaries, recursive
substitution, and the selection of pair products are established ingredients;
the issue addressed here is how to make them sharp for mixed signs and
selective for a quantum representation.

Formally, a quadratic function $F(x,y)$ is a pointwise-exact quadratization of
$f$ when
\begin{equation}
    f(x)=\min_{y\in\{0,1\}^{m}}F(x,y)
    \qquad\text{for every }x\in\{0,1\}^{n}.
    \label{eq:intro-pointwise}
\end{equation}
This condition, used throughout the quadratization literature
~\cite{BorosGruber2014,AnthonyEtAl2017,CramaEtAl2025}, is stronger than
preserving only the global optimum: the lower envelope over auxiliary fibres
recovers the value of the original objective at every original assignment.
It does not, however, imply that the enlarged Hamiltonian has the same excited
spectrum or finite-depth QAOA dynamics.  Inconsistent auxiliary assignments
remain computational-basis states and receive penalty-dependent energies.
Consequently, pointwise exactness fixes the classical objective represented on
the original variables, but it does not decide whether the representation is a
good quantum circuit.

This distinction has become increasingly important in quantum toolchains.
Different complete quadratizations of the same higher-order objective can
change the qubit count, interaction density, logical gate count, and
unoptimised cost-layer depth~\cite{Schmidbauer2024}.  Global substitution
selection has also been used to reduce interaction-graph degree and a resulting
depth bound for constrained QAOA formulations~\cite{Herrman2021}; graph-based selection
can accelerate the transformation and control structural properties of the
resulting QUBO~\cite{Schmidbauer2026}.  Rovara, Burgholzer, and Wille go one
step further and choose auxiliaries that induce interaction graphs compiling
efficiently on sparse devices, obtaining lower transpiled depth at the cost of
additional qubits~\cite{Rovara2026}.  These methods commit to complete
quadratization.  A complementary inexact approach replaces a higher-order cost
generator by an ancilla-free quadratic ansatz, sacrificing noiseless fidelity
for shorter and more noise-tolerant circuits~\cite{Dragoi2026}.  It does not
produce a pointwise-exact mixed representation.

The remaining design question is therefore different: if exactness is held
fixed, which interactions should remain native and which should be reduced?
To the best of our knowledge, the existing literature does not jointly choose
(i) the higher-order terms retained in native form, (ii) reusable product
auxiliaries for the reduced terms, (iii) pointwise necessary-and-sufficient
penalties for the aggregate residuals attached to those auxiliaries, and
(iv) the representation according to device-specific compiled costs and
relaxation information.  Most closely on the classical side,
Verma--Lewis--Kochenberger optimise shared pair substitutions for a complete
cubic-to-QUBO transformation~\cite{VermaLewisKochenberger2021}.  Our penalty
analysis instead maximises the collected residual over all Boolean assignments;
this is essential when positive and negative coefficients sharing a pair can
be activated independently.  On the quantum side, previous comparisons either
contrast endpoint encodings, require full quadratization, or deliberately relax
exactness.

We address this gap with \emph{exact selective locality reduction}.  Only a
chosen family of higher-order terms is reduced; all other interactions retain
their original locality.  An auxiliary may be shared by several selected
terms, and its penalty is determined from their collected residual rather than
from any single coefficient.  The resulting design space contains the
all-native and fully quadratized representations as endpoints, together with
pointwise-exact mixed representations between them.  Exactness is required to
compose through a sequence of substitutions: if $g^{(R)}$ is the objective
after $R$ steps, then
\begin{equation}
    f(x)=\min_{y_1,\ldots,y_R\in\{0,1\}}
          g^{(R)}(x,y_1,\ldots,y_R)
    \qquad\text{for every }x\in\{0,1\}^{n}.
    \label{eq:intro-composition}
\end{equation}
This gives a common classical lower envelope on the original variables while
allowing the enlarged spectra and compiled circuits to differ across
representations.

Relaxation-based warm starts provide a complementary source of structure.
Egger, Mare\v{c}ek, and Woerner map a continuous-relaxation solution to a
separable initial state and corresponding local mixers~\cite{Egger2021}.
For Max-Cut, Tate et al. use low-rank semidefinite relaxations to construct
biased initial superpositions~\cite{TateSDP2023}, and subsequently pair
arbitrary separable warm starts with custom mixers, establishing convergence in
the adiabatic limit and reporting low-depth improvements in their benchmark
settings~\cite{TateCustom2023}.  These constructions assume a fixed quantum
register.  Under selective reduction the register itself depends on the
representation: every active product auxiliary needs a relaxation-derived
marginal.  We therefore use one common relaxation to initialise both original
and active auxiliary qubits, to define the associated local mixers, and to
measure fibre inconsistency during representation selection.  This is a
consistency-aware, ancilla-adapted use of established warm-start ideas, rather
than a new warm-start family.

The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For an arbitrary-degree current pseudo-Boolean objective and a fixed
    Rosenberg product substitution, we prove that
    $M\geq\lVert A\rVert_{\infty}$ is necessary and sufficient for pointwise
    exactness, where $A$ is the \emph{collected aggregate residual} of every
    substituted term sharing the auxiliary.  The threshold is therefore sharp
    for the one-step substitution and treats shared terms jointly.

    \item We formalise a telescoping composition theorem for successive
    substitutions.  It yields a pointwise-exact procedure for reducing any
    selected family of monomials to a prescribed target locality, while making
    clear that substitution order can change thresholds, auxiliary reuse, and
    compiled resources even when correctness is preserved.

    \item For cubic objectives, the aggregate residual is linear.  We obtain a
    closed-form mixed-sign threshold equal to the larger of the total positive
    weight and the absolute total negative weight attached to a shared pair.
    This defines a finite family of exact mixed quadratic--cubic
    representations containing both locality endpoints.

    \item We formulate compiler- and relaxation-aware selection over this exact
    family.  The declared objective combines auxiliary-qubit count, compiled
    two-qubit gate count and depth, and maximum penalty scale, while
    relaxation-derived fibre excess is used as a guardrail or Pareto
    coordinate.  A finite anytime search returns a feasible incumbent whenever
    one is found and, in certification mode, a valid optimality-gap bound.

    \item We use the same relaxation to initialise original and active
    auxiliary qubits and to construct the corresponding mixers.  We then
    compare all-native, fully quadratized, selectively reduced, and
    matched-random representations under common compilation and optimisation
    budgets, so that the numerical study tests representation choice rather
    than an uncontrolled change of objective.
\end{enumerate}

The selection routine is intended to operationalise a controlled comparison of
quantum representations, not to introduce a specialised classical optimiser
for the design problem.  The remainder of the paper is organised as follows.
Section~2 introduces the pseudo-Boolean, circuit, and relaxation notation.
Section~3 develops the general selective target-locality theory and its cubic
specialisation.  Section~4 formulates representation selection, and Section~5
constructs the auxiliary-aware warm start.  Sections~6 and~7 describe the
benchmark families and experimental protocol.  Sections~8 and~9 report and
discuss the results, limitations, and representation-regime map.



\section{Problem setting and notation}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{Pseudo-Boolean objectives and diagonal encoding}
\label{sec:pb-diagonal}

Write \(\mathbb{B}:=\{0,1\}\), \([n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and
\(x_A:=\prod_{i\in A}x_i\), with \(x_\varnothing:=1\).  Every
pseudo-Boolean function \(f:\mathbb{B}^n\to\mathbb{R}\) has the unique
multilinear representation
\begin{equation}
  f(x)=\sum_{A\subseteq[n]}c_Ax_A,
  \qquad
  c_A=\sum_{B\subseteq A}(-1)^{|A|-|B|}f(\mathbf{1}_B),
  \label{eq:canonical-pb}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf{1}_B\) is the characteristic vector of \(B\).  This standard
fact is recalled in Appendix~\ref{app:canonical-pb}
~\cite{BorosHammer2002,Hadfield2021}.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{msupp}(f):=\{A\subseteq[n]:c_A\neq0\},
  \qquad
  \deg(f):=\max_{A\in\operatorname{msupp}(f)}|A|.
  \label{eq:support-degree}
\end{equation}
For a target locality \(\ell\geq2\), let
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}_{>\ell}(f):=
  \{A\in\operatorname{msupp}(f):|A|>\ell\},
  \qquad
  f=f_{\leq\ell}+f_{>\ell},
  \label{eq:target-split}
\end{equation}
where the two summands collect the terms of degree at most \(\ell\) and greater
than \(\ell\), respectively.  Selective locality reduction acts only on a
chosen subfamily of \(\mathcal{M}_{>\ell}(f)\); \(\ell=2\) gives selective
quadratization.

Let \(N_i:=(I-Z_i)/2\), \(N_A:=\prod_{i\in A}N_i\), and define the diagonal
cost Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
  H_f:=f(N_1,\ldots,N_n)=\sum_{A\subseteq[n]}c_AN_A,
  \qquad
  H_f\lvert x\rangle=f(x)\lvert x\rangle.
  \label{eq:diagonal-cost}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\lambda_{\min}(H_f)=\min_x f(x)\), and the cost unitary is
\begin{equation}
  U_f(\gamma):=e^{-i\gamma H_f},
  \qquad
  U_f(\gamma)\lvert x\rangle=e^{-i\gamma f(x)}\lvert x\rangle.
  \label{eq:phase-separator}
\end{equation}
This Boolean-to-Hamiltonian map and its use as a QAOA phase separator are
standard~\cite{Hadfield2021,Farhi2014}.
The expansion \(N_A=2^{-|A|}\sum_{B\subseteq A}(-1)^{|B|}Z_B\) shows that
the maximum Pauli weight equals \(\deg(f)\); details are in
Appendix~\ref{app:pauli-expansion}.  Consequently, retaining a degree-\(k\)
term leaves a logical \(k\)-qubit phase interaction to be compiled, whereas
reducing it trades locality for auxiliary qubits and penalty couplings.  We do
not assume a native hardware implementation of higher-order interactions
~\cite{CampbellDahl2022,Schmidbauer2024}.

\subsection{Pointwise-exact auxiliary reformulations}
\label{sec:pointwise-exactness}

Let \(y\in\mathbb{B}^m\) denote auxiliary variables.  A reformulation
\(F:\mathbb{B}^{n+m}\to\mathbb{R}\) is \emph{pointwise exact} when
\begin{equation}
  f(x)=\min_{y\in\mathbb{B}^m}F(x,y)
  \qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{B}^n.
  \label{eq:pointwise-exactness}
\end{equation}
It is a quadratization when, additionally, \(\deg(F)\leq2\); a selective
reduction may retain higher-order terms
~\cite{BorosGruber2014,AnthonyEtAl2017,CramaEtAl2025}.  For fixed \(x\), define
the optimal auxiliary fibre
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Y}_F(x):=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{y\in\mathbb{B}^m}F(x,y).
  \label{eq:optimal-fibre}
\end{equation}
If \(\mathcal{C}(x)\) is the set of assignments satisfying the intended
auxiliary relations, the reformulation is \emph{constraint-faithful} when
\(\mathcal{Y}_F(x)\subseteq\mathcal{C}(x)\) for every \(x\).  When those
relations define a single encoding \(\phi(x)\), strict consistency means
\(\mathcal{Y}_F(x)=\{\phi(x)\}\).

Pointwise exactness is stronger than preservation of the global optimum.  It
implies
\begin{equation}
  F(x,y)\geq f(x),
  \qquad
  \min_{x,y}F(x,y)=\min_x f(x),
  \qquad
  \pi_x\!\left(\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{x,y}F(x,y)\right)
  =\operatorname*{arg\,min}_x f(x),
  \label{eq:pointwise-consequences}
\end{equation}
as proved in Appendix~\ref{app:pointwise-consequences}.  It does not identify
the excited-state spectra or finite-depth QAOA dynamics of \(H_f\) and \(H_F\),
because \(H_F\) also assigns energies to inconsistent auxiliary states.

For binary \(a,b,y\), the Rosenberg product penalty~\cite{Rosenberg1975} is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm R}(a,b,y):=ab-2ay-2by+3y,
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm R}(a,b,y)\geq0,
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm R}(a,b,y)=0\iff y=ab.
  \label{eq:rosenberg}
\end{equation}
Its truth table is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:rosenberg-table}.
Section~\ref{sec:cubic-quadratization}
derives the sharp penalty required when one product auxiliary is shared by
several terms and proves exactness under successive substitutions.

\subsection{Representation, resource, and relaxation data}
\label{sec:resource-relaxation-data}

Let \(\sigma\) denote an exact native, selective, or fully reduced
representation,
\begin{equation}
  F_\sigma(u)=\sum_{S\subseteq[N_\sigma]}c_S^{(\sigma)}u_S,
  \qquad
  N_\sigma=n+a_\sigma,
  \qquad
  L_\sigma:=\max_{c_S^{(\sigma)}\neq0}|S|,
  \label{eq:representation-data}
\end{equation}
where \(a_\sigma\) is the number of auxiliaries.  Its explicit quadratic
couplings form
\begin{equation}
  G_{2,\sigma}:=([N_\sigma],E_{2,\sigma}),
  \qquad
  E_{2,\sigma}:=
  \bigl\{\{i,j\}:c_{\{i,j\}}^{(\sigma)}\neq0\bigr\};
  \label{eq:quadratic-graph}
\end{equation}
higher-order terms are retained as hyperedges of their corresponding supports.

To compare such representations after synthesis, we use resource coordinates
motivated by compilation-based QAOA studies
~\cite{Weidenfeller2022,Schmidbauer2024,Rovara2026}.
Fixing an architecture \(\mathcal{A}\) and a complete compilation protocol
\(\mathcal{C}\), one compiled cost layer produces the deterministic resource
triple
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Compile}_{\mathcal A,\mathcal C}(F_\sigma)
  =\bigl(G_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathcal A,\mathcal C},
         D_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathcal A,\mathcal C},
         S_\sigma^{\mathcal A,\mathcal C}\bigr),
  \label{eq:compiled-resources}
\end{equation}
denoting two-qubit gate count, two-qubit depth, and routing-SWAP count.  If the
active exact penalties are \(\{M_{r,\sigma}\}\), set
\(M_{\max,\sigma}:=\max_r M_{r,\sigma}\), with value zero when no penalty is
present.  Writing the collected diagonal Hamiltonian as
\(H_{F_\sigma}=\sum_T h_T^{(\sigma)}Z_T\), define
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_\sigma:=
  \frac{\max_{T\neq\varnothing,\,h_T^{(\sigma)}\neq0}|h_T^{(\sigma)}|}
       {\min_{T\neq\varnothing,\,h_T^{(\sigma)}\neq0}|h_T^{(\sigma)}|}.
  \label{eq:pauli-dynamic-range}
\end{equation}
This ratio is invariant under global Hamiltonian rescaling.

Finally, a tractable relaxation supplies original-variable marginals
\(\mu_i\in[0,1]\) and, when available, lifted product marginals
\(q_p\in[0,1]\) for candidate auxiliaries.  These data are used by the
representation selector in Section~\ref{sec:selection} and by the warm-start
construction in Section~\ref{sec:warm-start}, which builds on
relaxation-informed QAOA warm starts
~\cite{Egger2021,TateSDP2023,TateCustom2023}; neither independence nor exact
realizability is assumed here.

\section{Selective Locality Reduction and Cubic Quadratization}
\label{sec:cubic-quadratization}

The preceding section fixes the notation for pseudo-Boolean objectives and
pointwise-exact auxiliary-variable reformulations.  We first establish two
general results that apply to objectives of arbitrary degree: a sharp penalty
condition for one product substitution, and a composition rule for successive
substitutions.  Together they give an exact procedure for reducing any selected
family of monomials to a prescribed target locality.  We then specialise the
construction to cubic objectives, for which a single pair substitution is
sufficient and the penalty threshold admits a closed form.

The construction builds on established Rosenberg-style product substitutions,
recursive monomial decomposition, and auxiliary reuse
~\cite{Rosenberg1975,BorosGruber2014,VermaLewisKochenberger2021,CramaEtAl2025};
the sharp aggregate-residual threshold and selective composition developed
below are the present contributions.


\subsection{General one-step product substitution}
\label{sec:one-step-substitution}

Let \(q:\mathbb{B}^N\to\mathbb{R}\) be the current collected multilinear
objective; its coordinates \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_N)\) may include auxiliaries from
earlier steps, and \(u_T:=\prod_{k\in T}u_k\).  Fix
\(p=\{i,j\}\subseteq[N]\) and a family
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}_p\subseteq
  \{S\in\operatorname{msupp}(q):p\subseteq S,\ |S|\geq3\}
  \label{eq:selected-family-one-step}
\end{equation}
of monomials whose common factor \(u_i u_j\) is to be replaced.  Writing
\(v:=(u_k)_{k\notin p}\), collect these terms as
\begin{equation}
  q(u)=B_p(u)+u_i u_j A_p(v),
  \qquad
  A_p(v):=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_p}d_Su_{S\setminus p},
  \qquad
  B_p:=q-u_i u_jA_p.
  \label{eq:aggregate-residual-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Introduce \(y_p\in\mathbb{B}\), intended to satisfy \(y_p=u_i u_j\), and set
\begin{equation}
  Q_{p,M_p}(u,y_p)
  :=B_p(u)+y_pA_p(v)+M_pP_{\mathrm R}(u_i,u_j,y_p),
  \qquad
  \|A_p\|_\infty:=\max_{v\in\mathbb{B}^{N-2}}|A_p(v)|.
  \label{eq:one-step-reformulation}
\end{equation}
The reuse of one pair auxiliary across several terms has appeared in complete
cubic-to-QUBO transformations~\cite{VermaLewisKochenberger2021}.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp aggregate-residual penalty]
\label{prop:sharp-aggregate-residual}
For \(M_p\geq0\),
\begin{equation}
  q(u)=\min_{y_p\in\mathbb{B}}Q_{p,M_p}(u,y_p)
  \quad\text{for every }u\in\mathbb{B}^N
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  M_p\geq\|A_p\|_\infty.
  \label{eq:sharp-one-step-threshold}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every \((u,y_p)\),
\begin{equation}
  Q_{p,M_p}(u,y_p)-q(u)
  \geq
  \bigl(M_p-\|A_p\|_\infty\bigr)
  \mathbf{1}_{\{y_p\neq u_i u_j\}}.
  \label{eq:one-step-margin}
\end{equation}
Hence \(y_p=u_i u_j\) is the unique fibre minimiser for every \(u\) if and
only if \(M_p>\|A_p\|_\infty\).  The proof is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:sharp-one-step-proof}.
\end{proposition}

The threshold depends on the aggregate contribution of all terms sharing
\(y_p\), not on their largest individual coefficient.  In a sequential
reduction, \(A_p\) must therefore be recomputed from the current collected
objective before each substitution.
\subsection{Compositional exactness}
\label{sec:compositional-exactness}

Starting from \(g^{(0)}:=f\), let
\(y_{<r}:=(y_1,\ldots,y_{r-1})\) and
\(u^{(r-1)}:=(x,y_{<r})\) be the current variables before step \(r\).
Choose two distinct current coordinates \(a_r,b_r\), collect any selected
family of current monomials sharing their product, and write
\begin{equation}
  g^{(r-1)}(u^{(r-1)})
  =B_r(u^{(r-1)})+a_rb_rA_r(\widehat u_r),
  \qquad
  g^{(r)}(u^{(r-1)},y_r)
  :=B_r+y_rA_r+M_rP_{\mathrm R}(a_r,b_r,y_r),
  \label{eq:sequential-substitution}
\end{equation}
where \(y_r\) is fresh and \(\widehat u_r\) omits the two selected
coordinates.  The aggregate residual \(A_r\) is recomputed from the
\emph{current collected polynomial}, treating all current variables as
independent Boolean coordinates.  Set
\begin{equation}
  \delta_r:=M_r-\|A_r\|_\infty,
  \qquad
  \phi_r(x):=a_r(x,\phi_{<r}(x))\,b_r(x,\phi_{<r}(x)),
  \qquad
  \Phi(x):=(\phi_1(x),\ldots,\phi_R(x)).
  \label{eq:sequential-margin-and-lift}
\end{equation}
Here \(\phi_{<1}\) is the empty tuple.

\begin{theorem}[Sequential compositional exactness]
\label{thm:sequential-compositional-exactness}
If \(M_r\geq\|A_r\|_\infty\) for every \(r\in[R]\), then, for all
\((x,y)\in\mathbb B^{n+R}\),
\begin{equation}
  g^{(R)}(x,y)-f(x)
  \geq
  \sum_{r=1}^R\delta_r
  \mathbf 1_{\{y_r\neq
  a_r(x,y_{<r})b_r(x,y_{<r})\}}
  \geq0,
  \label{eq:compositional-gap}
\end{equation}
and, for every \(x\in\mathbb B^n\),
\begin{equation}
  f(x)=\min_{y\in\mathbb B^R}g^{(R)}(x,y)
      =g^{(R)}(x,\Phi(x)).
  \label{eq:compositional-exactness}
\end{equation}
If all inequalities are strict, then
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{y\in\mathbb B^R}g^{(R)}(x,y)
  =\{\Phi(x)\}
  \qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb B^n.
  \label{eq:strict-fibre-uniqueness}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof is the telescoping application of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-aggregate-residual} and is deferred to
Appendix~\ref{app:compositional-proof}.  At a non-strict threshold the
consistent lift still attains the exact lower envelope, although an
inconsistent assignment may tie.  Strict local thresholds are a convenient
sufficient---not necessary---condition for a unique auxiliary fibre.  The
theorem applies to every admissible
topological substitution order.  Thus correctness is order independent,
whereas the extended polynomial, thresholds, reuse pattern, and compiled
resources need not be.

\begin{corollary}[Selective target-\(\ell\) reduction]
\label{cor:selective-target-locality}
Let \(f(x)=\sum_{A\subseteq[n]}c_Ax_A\), let
\(\mathcal D\subseteq\operatorname{msupp}(f)\), and fix \(\ell\geq2\).
There is an acyclic Rosenberg reduction \(F_{\mathcal D,\ell}\) such that
\begin{equation}
  f(x)=\min_{y\in\mathbb B^m}F_{\mathcal D,\ell}(x,y),
  \qquad
  m\leq\sum_{A\in\mathcal D}\max\{|A|-\ell,0\},
  \label{eq:target-locality-bound}
\end{equation}
and every selected monomial has a terminal representation of degree at most
\(\ell\).  A disjoint fresh-chain construction permits
\(M_{A,s}\geq|c_A|\); strict inequalities give the unique recursively
consistent lift.  If \(\mathcal M_{>\ell}(f)\subseteq\mathcal D\), then
\(\deg(F_{\mathcal D,\ell})\leq\ell\).
\end{corollary}

This is a Rosenberg-style recursive monomial decomposition
~\cite{Rosenberg1975,BorosGruber2014,CramaEtAl2025};
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-aggregate-residual} supplies its sharp stepwise
calibration.
The construction and proof are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:target-locality-proof}.  Shared intermediate products can
reduce the auxiliary count in Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-locality-bound}, but then
the current aggregate residuals, and hence the sharp penalties, must be
recomputed.

\subsection{Cubic specialisation}
\label{sec:cubic-specialisation}

Assume $\deg(f)\le 3$ and write
\begin{equation}
f(x)=f_{\le 2}(x)+\sum_{A\in\mathcal C_3(f)} c_Ax_A,
\qquad
\mathcal C_3(f):=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|=3,\ c_A\neq0\}.
\label{eq:cubic-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Choose a family $\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal C_3(f)$ to be reduced, let
$\mathcal R:=\mathcal C_3(f)\setminus\mathcal D$, and assign each
$A\in\mathcal D$ a constituent pair
\(
\rho(A)\in\binom{A}{2}.
\)
Set
\[
P_\rho:=\rho(\mathcal D),
\qquad
\mathcal D_p:=\{A\in\mathcal D:\rho(A)=p\},
\qquad
A_p(x):=\sum_{A\in\mathcal D_p} c_Ax_{A\setminus p}.
\]
For each $p=\{i,j\}\in P_\rho$, introduce $y_p$ with intended relation
$y_p=x_ix_j$. The resulting mixed representation is
\begin{equation}
F_{\rho,M}(x,y)
=
f_{\le2}(x)
+\sum_{A\in\mathcal R}c_Ax_A
+\sum_{p\in P_\rho}y_pA_p(x)
+\sum_{p=\{i,j\}, p\in P_\rho}
M_pP_{\mathrm R}(x_i,x_j,y_p).
\label{eq:cubic-mixed-objective}
\end{equation}

Shared-pair substitutions are established in complete cubic reductions
~\cite{VermaLewisKochenberger2021}; here they are used selectively and
calibrated by the mixed-sign threshold below.

Because every $A\in\mathcal D_p$ is cubic, $A_p$ is linear. Define
\begin{equation}
C_p^+
:=
\sum_{A\in\mathcal D_p,\,c_A>0}c_A,
\qquad
C_p^-
:=
\sum_{A\in\mathcal D_p, \,c_A<0}|c_A|,
\qquad
T_p:=\max\{C_p^+,C_p^-\}.
\label{eq:cubic-threshold}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact cubic mixed-sign reduction]
\label{prop:cubic-mixed-sign}
For the representation \eqref{eq:cubic-mixed-objective},
\(
\|A_p\|_\infty=T_p,
\)
and
\begin{equation}
f(x)=\min_y F_{\rho,M}(x,y)
\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb B^n
\label{eq:cubic-pointwise-exactness}
\end{equation}
if and only if
\[
M_p\ge T_p
\qquad\forall p\in P_\rho.
\]
If all inequalities are strict, then the unique auxiliary minimiser is
\(
\phi_\rho(x):=(x_ix_j)_{p=\{i,j\}\in P_\rho}.
\)
\end{proposition}

The proof is a direct specialisation of Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-aggregate-residual}:
for linear $A_p$, its maximum and minimum are $C_p^+$ and $-C_p^-$,
respectively. 
Hence a design $\sigma=(\mathcal D,\rho)$ together with canonical penalties
\begin{equation}
M_p=T_p+\varepsilon_p,
\qquad
\text{with fixed }\varepsilon_p>0,
\label{eq:canonical-cubic-penalty}
\end{equation}
defines a pointwise-exact mixed quadratic--cubic representation with a unique
consistent auxiliary lift. It introduces $|P_\rho|$ auxiliaries and is fully
quadratic precisely when $\mathcal D=\mathcal C_3(f)$.
Structural characterisations of auxiliary reuse and an optional linear
encoding of the exact design space are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:cubic-structure}.
Let $\Sigma_{\mathrm{ex}}^\varepsilon$ denote the finite family of all
representations constructed in this way.


\section{Resource-Aware Representation Selection}
\label{sec:selection}

Section~\ref{sec:cubic-quadratization} defines a finite family
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{ex}}^\varepsilon$ of
pointwise-exact mixed quadratic--cubic representations. We now select among
these representations using compiled circuit resources and a
relaxation-derived fibre-risk guardrail. Exactness is therefore fixed
throughout the design space and is not traded against hardware cost.

Previous work has shown that the choice of polynomial reduction can materially
change QAOA circuit resources~\cite{Schmidbauer2024}, and has considered
structural or compiler-aware auxiliary selection for fully quadratized
formulations~\cite{Herrman2021,Schmidbauer2026,Rovara2026}.  In contrast, the
present design space retains a native action for every cubic term and therefore
also contains exact mixed-locality representations.

\subsection{Representation-selection problem}
\label{sec:selection-problem}

A design is $\sigma=(\mathcal D,\rho)\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{ex}}^\varepsilon$,
with canonical penalties
\(
M_p=T_p(\rho)+\varepsilon_p,\, \varepsilon_p>0,
\)
as in Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-mixed-sign}. Hence every candidate satisfies
\[
f(x)=\min_y F_\sigma(x,y),
\qquad
\arg\min_y F_\sigma(x,y)=\{\phi_\rho(x)\}.
\]
The all-native and fully quadratized representations are included as endpoint
designs.

Using the deterministic compilation protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:resource-relaxation-data}, let
\[
\operatorname{Compile}_{\Pi}(F_\sigma)
=
(G_{2q,\sigma},D_{2q,\sigma},S_\sigma),
\qquad
a_\sigma:=|P_\rho|,
\]
and set
\(
M_{\max,\sigma}:=
\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{M_p:p\in P_\rho\}\bigr).
\)
For fixed positive scales $s_a,s_G,s_D,s_M$ and non-negative weights
$w_a+w_G+w_D+w_M=1$, define
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{res}}(\sigma)
=
w_a\frac{a_\sigma}{s_a}
+w_G\frac{G_{2q,\sigma}}{s_G}
+w_D\frac{D_{2q,\sigma}}{s_D}
+w_M\frac{M_{\max,\sigma}}{s_M}.
\label{eq:Jres}
\end{equation}

We evaluate $G_{2q,\sigma}$ and $D_{2q,\sigma}$ after the declared synthesis
and compilation protocol, rather than using polynomial locality or a
pre-routing logical-depth proxy alone.  Phase-gadget synthesis and ordering,
device connectivity, and routing can all materially change the realised QAOA
cost-layer depth~\cite{Verchere2023,Weidenfeller2022,Pelofske2024,Rovara2026}.

Let $(\mu,q)$ be fixed relaxation data shared by all candidate
representations. Under the associated product distribution define
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma;\mu,q)
:=
\mathbb E\!\left[F_\sigma(X,Y)-f(X)\right]\ge0,
\qquad
\widehat E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma)
:=
\frac{E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma;\mu,q)}{s_{\mathrm{fib}}},
\label{eq:fibre-excess-short}
\end{equation}
where $s_{\mathrm{fib}}>0$ is fixed independently of $\sigma$.
The closed form and auxiliary diagnostics are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:fibre-risk}.

The use of relaxation marginals is motivated by relaxation-based warm-start
QAOA~\cite{Egger2021,TateSDP2023}; the fibre-excess quantity introduced here is
instead a representation-dependent diagnostic of auxiliary inconsistency.

For declared budgets, let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}
:=
\left\{
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{ex}}^\varepsilon:
N_\sigma\le Q_{\max},\
G_{2q,\sigma}\le B_G,\
D_{2q,\sigma}\le B_D,\
M_{\max,\sigma}\le B_M,\
\widehat E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma)\le\tau_{\mathrm{fib}}
\right\}.
\label{eq:selection-feasible-set}
\end{equation}
Additional declared hardware constraints, such as coefficient dynamic range
or routing limits, may be imposed without changing the formulation. The ideal representation-selection problem is therefore
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star
:=
\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}}
J_{\mathrm{res}}(\sigma),
\qquad
\sigma^\star\in
\arg\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}}
J_{\mathrm{res}}(\sigma).
\label{eq:selection-problem}
\end{equation}
The corresponding empirical Pareto archive is retained as a
weight-independent diagnostic; its precise ordering rule is deferred to
Appendix~\ref{app:selector-details}.  Its non-dominated ranking and
crowding-distance tie-break follow standard NSGA-II practice
~\cite{DebEtAl2002}.


\subsection{Search and certification}
\label{sec:search-certification}

Let $\mathcal C_3(f)=\{A_1,\ldots,A_m\}$ be ordered. Each cubic term has four
possible actions,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal U_t
=
\{\bot\}\cup\binom{A_t}{2},
\label{eq:action-set}
\end{equation}
where $\bot$ retains $A_t$ natively and $p\in\binom{A_t}{2}$ reduces it
through $p$. Thus the complete design space contains $4^m$ leaves.

A finite-width Pareto beam is used to generate good feasible incumbents under
a prescribed compilation budget. When certification is requested, an
anytime branch-and-bound search is subsequently applied to the same ordered
design tree. For each unresolved state $s$, let $L_J(s)$ be any verified
lower bound satisfying
\begin{equation}
L_J(s)
\le
\min_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Desc}(s)\cap\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}}
J_{\mathrm{res}}(\sigma).
\label{eq:admissible-bound}
\end{equation}
The explicit bound, cache rules, beam ranking, and frontier updates are given
in Appendix~\ref{app:selector-details}.

Let $U_J$ be the best feasible objective value found so far and
$\mathrm{Open}$ the complete unresolved frontier. Define
\begin{equation}
L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}
:=
\min\left(
U_J,\,
\min_{s\in\mathrm{Open}}L_J(s)
\right),
\label{eq:global-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
with the minimum over an empty frontier interpreted as $+\infty$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Compiler-aware representation selector}
\label{alg:selector}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Ordered cubic supports $(A_1,\ldots,A_m)$; exact candidate family
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{ex}}^\varepsilon$; deterministic protocol $\Pi$;
beam width $B$; flag $\mathrm{Certify}$.
\Ensure Incumbent $\sigma_{\mathrm{inc}}$ and value $U_J$;
if $\mathrm{Certify}=\mathrm{true}$, also a certified lower bound
$L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}$.
\State $X\gets(K,V,\mathcal P_{\mathrm{emp}},\sigma_{\mathrm{inc}},U_J)
      \gets(\varnothing,\varnothing,\varnothing,\mathrm{None},+\infty)$
      \Comment{cache, evaluated designs, Pareto archive, incumbent, incumbent value}
\State $X\gets\Call{EvaluateCandidate}{\sigma_{\mathrm{nat}},X,\Pi}$
\State $X\gets\Call{EvaluateCandidate}{\sigma_{\mathrm{fq}}^{\mathrm{ref}},X,\Pi}$
\State $X\gets\Call{ParetoBeam}{s_0,B,X,\Pi}$

\If{$\mathrm{Certify}$}
    \State $(X,\mathrm{Open},L_J^{\mathrm{glob}},\mathrm{status})
    \gets\Call{BranchAndBound}{s_0,X,\Pi}$
    \State \Return $(\sigma_{\mathrm{inc}},U_J,L_J^{\mathrm{glob}},
    \mathrm{status})$
\Else
    \State \Return $(\sigma_{\mathrm{inc}},U_J)$
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
The evaluation, beam-search, and certification subroutines are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:selector-details}. The beam generates incumbents only;
global certification relies exclusively on the complete branch-and-bound
frontier.


\begin{proposition}[Anytime selection certificate]
\label{prop:anytime-certificate}
Every candidate evaluated by Algorithm~\ref{alg:selector} is pointwise exact.
If the certification stage maintains the complete unresolved frontier and
prunes only by valid infeasibility tests or admissible subtree bounds, then
whenever $U_J<+\infty$,
\begin{equation}
L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}
\le
J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star
\le
U_J.
\label{eq:anytime-certificate}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
0\le
U_J-J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star
\le
U_J-L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}.
\]
In particular, $L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}=U_J$ certifies global optimality relative
to the declared design space, compiler protocol, budgets, and objective.
\end{proposition}
Implementation details are given in Appendices~\ref{app:selector-details}--~\ref{app:selection-implementation}, and the proof is
deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:anytime-certificate-proof}. Beam truncation is used only to generate
incumbents and never supports a global optimality claim.


\section{Warm-Started QAOA and Circuit Realisation}
\label{sec:warm-start}


\subsection{Representation-dependent warm start}
\label{sec:warm-start-state}

We adapt relaxation-informed warm-start QAOA
~\cite{Egger2021,TateSDP2023} to the selected original--auxiliary
representation.
Fix a selected exact representation
$\sigma=(\mathcal D,\rho,M)$ with active auxiliary pairs $P_\rho$.
Let $\bar\mu_i\in[0,1]$ be relaxation-derived original-variable marginals.
For a fixed $\delta\in(0,1/2)$, define
\[
\mu_i:=\operatorname{clip}_\delta(\bar\mu_i)
:=\min\{1-\delta,\max\{\delta,\bar\mu_i\}\}.
\]
For each $p=\{i,j\}\in P_\rho$, use
\[
q_p=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{clip}_\delta(\bar\mu_{ij}),
& \text{if a lifted pair marginal is available},\\
\mu_i\mu_j,
& \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
For $r\in[0,1]$, let
\[
|\phi(r)\rangle
:=
\sqrt{1-r}\,|0\rangle+\sqrt r\,|1\rangle
=
R_y(2\arcsin\sqrt r)\,|0\rangle .
\]
The representation-dependent warm start is
\begin{equation}
|\Phi_\sigma^{\mathrm{ws}}(\mu,q)\rangle
=
\bigotimes_{i=1}^n|\phi(\mu_i)\rangle
\otimes
\bigotimes_{p\in P_\rho}|\phi(q_p)\rangle .
\label{eq:ws-state}
\end{equation}

The same relaxation is used for every candidate representation; only the
required auxiliary marginals depend on $P_\rho$.
Although $q_p=\mu_i\mu_j$ matches the product marginal under the independence
closure, the product state in \eqref{eq:ws-state} does not enforce
$Y_p=X_iX_j$ sample-wise.  The corresponding fibre-risk diagnostics are
given in Appendix~\ref{app:fibre-risk}.

\subsection{Matched mixer and QAOA ansatz}
\label{sec:ws-qaoa}

Following matched and custom-mixer warm-start constructions
~\cite{Egger2021,TateCustom2023}, for a marginal $\omega\in(0,1)$ define
\[
h_M(\omega)
=
-\left[
2\sqrt{\omega(1-\omega)}\,X
+
(1-2\omega)Z
\right].
\]
Its unique ground state is $|\phi(\omega)\rangle$.  Applying this mixer
independently to the original and auxiliary registers gives
\begin{equation}
H_{M,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ws}}
=
\sum_{i=1}^n h_M(\mu_i)
+
\sum_{p\in P_\rho}h_M(q_p),
\label{eq:ws-mixer}
\end{equation}
whose unique ground state is
$|\Phi_\sigma^{\mathrm{ws}}(\mu,q)\rangle$.
Let
\[
H_{F_\sigma}:=F_\sigma(N_1,\ldots,N_{n+|P_\rho|}),
\qquad
U_{F_\sigma}(\gamma):=e^{-i\gamma H_{F_\sigma}},
\]
and
\[
U_{M,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ws}}(\beta)
:=
e^{-i\beta H_{M,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ws}}}.
\]
The resulting depth-$r$ warm-start QAOA state
~\cite{Farhi2014,Egger2021,TateCustom2023} is
\begin{equation}
|\psi_{r,\sigma}(\boldsymbol\gamma,\boldsymbol\beta)\rangle
=
U^{\mathrm{ws}}_{M,\sigma}(\beta_r)
U_{F_\sigma}(\gamma_r)
\cdots
U^{\mathrm{ws}}_{M,\sigma}(\beta_1)
U_{F_\sigma}(\gamma_1)
|\Phi^{\mathrm{ws}}_\sigma(\mu,q)\rangle .
\end{equation}

The local mixer does not preserve the auxiliary constraints
$y_p=x_ix_j$; inconsistent configurations remain accessible and are
energetically penalised by $H_{F_\sigma}$.
Single-qubit circuit realisations are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:warm-start-circuits}.



\subsection{Cost layer and consistency metric}
\label{sec:cost-consistency}
The selected pseudo-Boolean objective $F_\sigma$ is encoded through the
number-operator substitution $u_j\mapsto N_j=(I-Z_j)/2$
~\cite{Hadfield2021}.  Since all terms in
$H_{F_\sigma}$ are diagonal, the cost layer factorises into commuting phase
operators and introduces no Trotter error
~\cite{CampbellDahl2022,Verchere2023}.  Native cubic terms, reduced
quadratic interactions, and Rosenberg penalty blocks are compiled under the
same hardware and compiler protocol used in Section~\ref{sec:selection-problem}.
Explicit logical decompositions are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:warm-start-circuits}.

All representations use the same parameter-initialisation rule and classical
optimisation budget.  In addition to the objective value and optimal-solution
probability, we report
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{cons}}
:=
\Pr\!\left(
Y_p=X_iX_j\ \text{for every }p=\{i,j\}\in P_\rho
\right),
\label{eq:pcons}
\end{equation}
which distinguishes exploration of the auxiliary-inconsistent sector from
failure on the original optimisation problem.

\subsection{Max-3SAT}

Consider a clause $C=(\ell_i\lor\ell_j\lor\ell_k)$. Define the false-literal indicator
\begin{equation}
    u_{\ell_r}(x_r)
    :=\begin{cases}
       1-x_r, & \ell_r=x_r,\\
       x_r, & \ell_r=\neg x_r.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:false-indicator}
\end{equation}
The clause-violation penalty is
\begin{equation}
    P_C(\bm x)=u_{\ell_i}(x_i)u_{\ell_j}(x_j)u_{\ell_k}(x_k),
    \label{eq:clause-penalty}
\end{equation}
which equals one exactly when the clause is violated. A weighted Max-3SAT
instance therefore yields the cubic PUBO~\cite{Nagies2025,Nusslein2023}
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{3SAT}}(\bm x)=\sum_{C}w_CP_C(\bm x).
    \label{eq:max3sat-pubo}
\end{equation}
After coefficient aggregation, repeated variable pairs across clauses create opportunities for auxiliary reuse. The benchmark records clause density, pair-shadow size, overlap-graph statistics, coefficient cancellation, and the exact penalty profile. The same construction applies to every literal-sign pattern.

\subsubsection{A small worked Max-3SAT instance}
\label{sec:max3sat-worked-example}

To illustrate the mixed-sign reduction of \cref{prop:cubic-mixed-sign}, consider
the following weighted instance on five
Boolean variables:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        C_1&=(\neg x_1\lor\neg x_2\lor\neg x_3), & w_1&=5,\\
        C_2&=(x_2\lor x_3\lor x_4),              & w_2&=4,\\
        C_3&=(\neg x_1\lor\neg x_4\lor\neg x_5), & w_3&=2.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-clauses}
\end{equation}
The instance is constructed for illustration rather than as a difficult
Max-3SAT benchmark. Its three clause-violation penalties are
\begin{equation}
    P_{C_1}(\bm x)=x_1x_2x_3,\qquad
    P_{C_2}(\bm x)=(1-x_2)(1-x_3)(1-x_4),\qquad
    P_{C_3}(\bm x)=x_1x_4x_5.
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-clause-penalties}
\end{equation}
Hence the total violated weight of this example is
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{3SAT}}^{\mathrm{ex}}(\bm x)
    =5x_1x_2x_3
     +4(1-x_2)(1-x_3)(1-x_4)
     +2x_1x_4x_5.
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-objective}
\end{equation}
After expansion and coefficient aggregation,
\begin{align}
    f_{\mathrm{3SAT}}^{\mathrm{ex}}(\bm x)
    ={}&4-4x_2-4x_3-4x_4
       +4x_2x_3+4x_2x_4+4x_3x_4 \nonumber\\
      &+5x_1x_2x_3-4x_2x_3x_4+2x_1x_4x_5.
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-expanded}
\end{align}
Define the degree-at-most-two part
\begin{equation}
    g(\bm x)
    :=4-4x_2-4x_3-4x_4
      +4x_2x_3+4x_2x_4+4x_3x_4.
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-lower}
\end{equation}
Then the cubic component of \cref{eq:max3sat-worked-expanded} has the
mixed-sign structure covered by \cref{prop:cubic-mixed-sign}. Its supports and
admissible pair substitutions are shown in
\cref{tab:max3sat-worked-support}.
\begin{table}[t]
    \centering
    \caption{Cubic support of the worked Max-3SAT instance.}
    \label{tab:max3sat-worked-support}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}lcl@{}}
        \toprule
        Cubic support & Coefficient & Candidate pairs\\
        \midrule
        $\{1,2,3\}$ & $5$  & $\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}$\\

        $\{1,4,5\}$ & $2$  & $\{1,4\},\{1,5\},\{4,5\}$\\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}
The pair $\{2,3\}$ is shared by the first two cubic terms, while the third
term shares no pair with them.

\paragraph{All-native encoding.}
The all-native endpoint keeps all three cubic terms:
\begin{equation}
    F_{\mathrm{native}}(\bm x)
    =f_{\mathrm{3SAT}}^{\mathrm{ex}}(\bm x).
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-native}
\end{equation}
It uses five variables, introduces no auxiliary variables, and retains three
cubic interactions.

\paragraph{Fully quadratized encoding.}
For complete quadratization, introduce
\begin{equation}
    y_{23}=x_2x_3,
    \qquad
    y_{14}=x_1x_4.
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-full-auxiliaries}
\end{equation}
The first auxiliary is shared by the first two cubic terms, while the second
reduces the remaining term. For $p=\{2,3\}$, the aggregate residual is
\begin{equation}
    A_{23}(\bm x)=5x_1-4x_4,
    \qquad
    C_{23}^{+}=5,
    \qquad
    C_{23}^{-}=4.
\end{equation}
Therefore, \cref{prop:cubic-mixed-sign} gives the exact threshold
\begin{equation}
    M_{23}^{*}=\max\{5,4\}=5.
\end{equation}
For $p=\{1,4\}$, the aggregate residual is $A_{14}(\bm x)=2x_5$, so
\begin{equation}
    M_{14}^{*}=2.
\end{equation}
We use the unit positive margins $M_{23}=6$ and $M_{14}=3$ to make the
consistent auxiliary assignments unique. The fully quadratized objective is
\begin{align}
    F_{\mathrm{full}}(\bm x,y_{23},y_{14})
    ={}&g(\bm x)+5x_1y_{23}-4y_{23}x_4+2y_{14}x_5 \nonumber\\
      &+6\PR(x_2,x_3,y_{23})
       +3\PR(x_1,x_4,y_{14}).
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-full}
\end{align}
This representation contains no cubic terms and uses two auxiliary variables.

\paragraph{Selective encoding.}
A natural selective design reduces the two terms that share $\{2,3\}$ and
retains the isolated term $2x_1x_4x_5$. It is
\begin{align}
    F_{\mathrm{sel}}(\bm x,y_{23})
    ={}&g(\bm x)+5x_1y_{23}-4y_{23}x_4+2x_1x_4x_5 \nonumber\\
      &+6\PR(x_2,x_3,y_{23}).
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-selective}
\end{align}
This representation uses one auxiliary variable, replaces two cubic terms by
quadratic terms, and retains one native cubic term.

\paragraph{Exactness verification.}
We exhaustively enumerated all $2^5=32$ assignments of the original variables
and all assignments of the auxiliary variables. No pointwise mismatch was
found:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{3SAT}}^{\mathrm{ex}}(\bm x)
    =\min_{y_{23}\in\bits}F_{\mathrm{sel}}(\bm x,y_{23})
    =\min_{(y_{23},y_{14})\in\bits^2}
       F_{\mathrm{full}}(\bm x,y_{23},y_{14})
    \qquad\forall\bm x\in\bits^5.
    \label{eq:max3sat-worked-verification}
\end{equation}
All three representations have optimal objective value zero.

\subsection{Cubic spin glasses}

Higher-order Ising and spin-glass models form established benchmarks for QAOA
and related quantum optimisation studies
~\cite{Basso2022,PelofskeShortDepth2024,Pelofske2024}.
Let $s_i\in\{-1,+1\}$ and consider
\begin{equation}
    E(\bm s)=c+\sum_i h_is_i+\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}s_is_j
      +\sum_{i<j<k}K_{ijk}s_is_js_k.
    \label{eq:cubic-spin}
\end{equation}
The main benchmark uses the pure cubic case $c=0$, $h_i=0$, and
$J_{ij}=0$.  Each active unordered hyperedge is sampled without replacement
and has an independent coefficient $K_{ijk}\in\{-1,+1\}$ with equal
probability.  We use target hyperedge loads $m_{\mathrm e}\simeq n$, $2n$,
and $4n$ to obtain low-, intermediate-, and high-load candidate pools.  A
uniform proposal provides the low-reuse baseline, while an anchor-pair
proposal deliberately makes several hyperedges share the same pair.  The
requested generator class is not used as the final regime label: the realised
canonical sparsity and reuse statistics are measured after Boolean conversion
and coefficient aggregation.

With $s_i=1-2x_i$, the energy becomes a pseudo-Boolean polynomial of degree at
most three.  No instance-dependent normalisation is applied before this
conversion.  If a common positive rescaling is needed for circuit execution,
the same scale is applied to every representation of the same raw instance and
the reported objective values are converted back to the original units.  The
complete conversion convention is given in \cref{app:spin-glass}.

\subsection{Why both families are needed}

Max-3SAT has structured coefficient signs and overlap inherited from clauses,
whereas cubic spin glasses permit direct control of hyperedge sparsity and pair
reuse.  Together they separate effects caused by the application model from
effects caused by the abstract cubic hypergraph.  The main structural axes are
sparsity, auxiliary reuse, and target-hardware connectivity.  Coefficient-sign
balance is recorded only as a diagnostic rather than varied as a separate
experimental axis.  This scope tests the regime in which a limited number of
costly native interactions can be replaced by a modest number of reusable
auxiliaries without turning large penalties and congested auxiliary couplings
into the dominant cost.

\subsection{Benchmark generation and structural coordinates}
\label{sec:benchmark-generation}

For weighted Max-3SAT, each clause contains three distinct variables, each
literal sign is sampled independently with probability $1/2$, duplicate signed
clauses are excluded, and the positive integer weight is sampled uniformly from
$\{1,2,3,4,5\}$.  Candidate pools use target clause loads
$m_{\mathrm C}/n\simeq2,4,6$.  As for the spin-glass family, both uniform and
anchor-pair proposals are used so that sparsity and reuse are not conflated by
construction.

All clauses or hyperedges are expanded and equal monomials are aggregated
before structural features are calculated.  Let $\mathcal C_3(f)$ denote the
remaining nonzero cubic supports and $m_3=|\mathcal C_3(f)|$.  We record the
canonical cubic density $m_3/\binom{n}{3}$ and the load $m_3/n$.  The pair shadow
$\partial_2\mathcal C_3$ is the set of all pairs contained in at least one
cubic support.  Pair reuse is summarised by
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{pair}}
  =1-\frac{|\partial_2\mathcal C_3|}{3m_3},
  \qquad m_3>0,
  \label{eq:pair-reuse-score}
\end{equation}
together with the pair degrees $d(p)$ and
$\Delta_2=\max_p d(p)$.  Hardware connectivity is not attached to the raw
instance: each representation is compiled later to the same all-to-all
reference and the same fixed sparse device topology.

Generation seeds, raw data, canonical coefficients, structural metadata, and
split assignments are stored in versioned manifests.  All representations of
one raw instance share the same instance identifier and split.  Instances are
divided into training, validation, and test sets before representation or QAOA
results are inspected; no test instance is removed because one representation
performs unexpectedly.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:experiments}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Benchmark tiers and frozen comparisons}
\label{sec:benchmark-tiers}

The benchmark is organised into three overlapping workload tiers.  Small
oracle instances support exhaustive verification of the algebra and sharp
penalties.  QAOA instances are restricted so that every representation in a
comparison fits the same width limit.  A broader compilation tier is used for
logical resources and routing trends, but is not used to claim asymptotic
scaling.  Within each family and tier, the versioned manifest uses a
$60/20/20$ training--validation--test split.  Selector settings and common
execution settings are chosen using only the training and validation splits;
the frozen test split is evaluated once for the reported comparisons.

For every raw instance we compare four representations: the all-native
objective, a deterministic fully quadratized reference, the selective design
returned by the method of \cref{sec:selection}, and a matched-random selective
baseline.  The random baseline is matched to the selected design's auxiliary
count or number of reduced cubic terms and is averaged over a fixed seed
bundle.  All derived representations retain the raw instance identifier so
that every reported contrast is paired.

\subsection{Exactness and penalty-tightness protocol}
\label{sec:exactness-protocol}

For each oracle instance, all assignments of the original variables are
enumerated.  For each reduced representation we also minimise over, and where
feasible enumerate, all auxiliary assignments.  We verify pointwise that
\begin{equation}
  f(\bm x)=\min_{\bm y}F_{\rho}(\bm x,\bm y)
  \quad\text{for every }\bm x,
  \label{eq:experimental-pointwise-check}
\end{equation}
and check that a strictly feasible penalty $M_p=T_p+\varepsilon_p$ makes the
consistent auxiliary assignment unique.  Tightness is tested at three declared
settings around the analytical threshold: below $T_p$, at $T_p$, and above
$T_p$.  The output records the maximum pointwise mismatch, the number of ties
at threshold, the number of inconsistent minimisers, and an explicit witness
whenever a below-threshold penalty fails.

\subsection{Logical and compiled resource protocol}
\label{sec:resource-protocol}

Before compilation we record the number of original and auxiliary variables,
retained cubic terms, nonzero quadratic couplings, maximum penalty, and
coefficient dynamic range.  Each representation is then compiled with the same
synthesis rules and seed bundle to an all-to-all reference architecture and to
one fixed realistic sparse coupling map.  The primary compiled quantities are
two-qubit gate count, two-qubit depth, and routing overhead, including the
number of inserted SWAP operations.  Package versions, basis gates, coupling
maps, optimisation level, routing method, and transpiler seeds are stored with
the run record so that a resource difference cannot be attributed to a changed
compiler configuration.

\subsection{Relaxation, selection, and warm starts}
\label{sec:experimental-relaxation}

One first-and-pair-moment relaxation is used consistently for every
representation.  In the implementation this is an SA/RLT level-2 relaxation,
which directly returns the single-variable moments $\mu_i$ and pair moments
$q_{ij}$.  The independence closure $q_{ij}=\mu_i\mu_j$ is retained only as a
single ablation.  Before interpreting warm-start results, we report the
distribution of $|\mu_i-1/2|$; instances whose marginals are almost uniform
carry little warm-start information and are identified as such.

Selector weights, feasibility limits, and search settings are fixed using the
training and validation data and are not retuned on the test set.  The warm-start
comparison contains exactly three initialisation policies: a cold-start
standard QAOA state, a warm start applied only to the original variables, and a
warm start applied to both original and auxiliary variables.  The last policy
uses the same pair moments as the primary relaxation, thereby testing whether
the additional auxiliary information justifies the enlarged warm start.

\subsection{QAOA evaluation under common budgets}
\label{sec:qaoa-evaluation}

Each representation is optimised independently but receives the same number of
classical objective evaluations and the same number of restarts.  We report
both equal-layer comparisons and comparisons under a common compiled two-qubit
gate budget; the latter prevents a lower-locality representation from gaining
an unreported circuit-cost advantage.  The final optimiser settings, depths,
shot counts where applicable, and seed bundles are frozen in a versioned run
configuration.

Samples from an augmented representation are first projected onto the original
bits and then scored with the original Max-3SAT or Ising objective.  Encoded
energy and auxiliary-consistency rates are reported as diagnostics, but they do
not replace this common score.  The analysis reports all scheduled runs and
their uncertainty rather than selecting only the best optimiser seed.

\subsection{Structure, noise, and statistical analysis}
\label{sec:structure-noise-protocol}

The regime analysis uses only the three predeclared drivers: canonical cubic
sparsity, pair reuse, and hardware connectivity.  Sign balance and coefficient
heterogeneity may be shown as diagnostics but are not promoted to additional
factorial axes.  Noiseless statevector experiments form the primary QAOA
comparison.  A predeclared representative subset is then evaluated at only a
small number of realistic noise levels and on the same sparse topology.  A
real-device demonstration may be added later, but is not required for the main
claims.

All representation contrasts are paired by raw instance.  We report the
per-instance distribution, mean or median paired difference, uncertainty
intervals, and the fraction of instances for which selective quadratization
helps, has little effect, or is detrimental.  Machine-readable per-instance
tables, frozen manifests, configuration hashes, and all random seeds accompany
the aggregate figures.

\section{Background identities and proofs}
\label{app:background-identities}

\subsection{Canonical multilinear representation}
\label{app:canonical-pb}

The following Boolean-lattice M\"obius representation is standard
~\cite{BorosHammer2002,Hadfield2021}.
\begin{proposition}[Canonical multilinear representation]
Every \(f:\mathbb{B}^n\to\mathbb{R}\) has a unique representation
\begin{equation}
  f(x)=\sum_{A\subseteq[n]}c_Ax_A,
  \qquad
  c_A=\sum_{B\subseteq A}(-1)^{|A|-|B|}f(\mathbf{1}_B).
  \label{eq:app-mobius}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(T\subseteq[n]\), evaluation at \(\mathbf{1}_T\) gives
\(\sum_{A\subseteq T}c_A\).  Substituting the coefficients in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-mobius} and reversing the finite sums yields
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_{A\subseteq T}c_A
  =\sum_{B\subseteq T}f(\mathbf{1}_B)
    \sum_{B\subseteq A\subseteq T}(-1)^{|A|-|B|} 
  =\sum_{B\subseteq T}f(\mathbf{1}_B)(1-1)^{|T\setminus B|}
   =f(\mathbf{1}_T).
\end{equation*}
Every Boolean vector is \(\mathbf{1}_T\) for a unique \(T\), proving
existence.  Uniqueness follows recursively from the triangular identities
\(f(\mathbf{1}_T)=\sum_{A\subseteq T}c_A\), or equivalently from Boolean-lattice
M\"obius inversion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pauli expansion and phase synthesis}
\label{app:pauli-expansion}

Under the standard Boolean-to-diagonal-Hamiltonian map
~\cite{Hadfield2021}, the collected coefficients are as follows.
\begin{proposition}[Collected Pauli coefficients]
Let \(f\) be given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical-pb} and set
\(H_f=f(N_1,\ldots,N_n)\).  Then
\begin{equation}
  H_f=\sum_{B\subseteq[n]}h_BZ_B,
  \qquad
  h_B=(-1)^{|B|}\sum_{A\supseteq B}\frac{c_A}{2^{|A|}}.
  \label{eq:app-pauli-coefficients}
\end{equation}
If \(f\) is nonconstant, the maximum nonzero Pauli weight equals \(\deg(f)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since
\begin{equation}
  N_A=\prod_{i\in A}\frac{I-Z_i}{2}
     =\frac{1}{2^{|A|}}
      \sum_{B\subseteq A}(-1)^{|B|}Z_B,
  \label{eq:app-projector-pauli}
\end{equation}
collecting the coefficient of each \(Z_B\) gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-pauli-coefficients}.  If \(|B|>\deg(f)\), no nonzero support
\(A\supseteq B\) exists, so \(h_B=0\).  Conversely, choose
\(A\in\operatorname{msupp}(f)\) with \(|A|=\deg(f)\).  No strict superset of
\(A\) has a nonzero coefficient, and hence
\(h_A=(-1)^{|A|}2^{-|A|}c_A\neq0\).
\end{proof}

All Pauli terms commute, so
\begin{equation}
  e^{-i\gamma H_f}
  =\prod_{B:h_B\neq0}e^{-i\gamma h_BZ_B}.
  \label{eq:app-commuting-phase}
\end{equation}
For \(B=\{q_1,\ldots,q_k\}\), a standard ancilla-free parity ladder computes
the parity onto one qubit, applies \(R_z(2\gamma h_B)\), and uncomputes it,
using \(2(k-1)\) CNOT gates before cross-term compilation optimizations.  This
is a logical all-to-all reference synthesis, not a lower bound on all
implementations~\cite{CampbellDahl2022,Verchere2023}.

\subsection{Consequences of pointwise exactness}
\label{app:pointwise-consequences}

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise-exact lower envelope]
Suppose
\begin{equation}
  f(x)=\min_{y\in\mathbb{B}^m}F(x,y)
  \qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{B}^n.
  \label{eq:app-pointwise-assumption}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
  F(x,y)&\geq f(x) &&\text{for every }(x,y),
  \label{eq:app-lower-envelope}\\
  \min_{x,y}F(x,y)&=\min_x f(x),
  \label{eq:app-ground-energy}\\
  \pi_x\!\left(\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{x,y}F(x,y)\right)
  &=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_x f(x).
  \label{eq:app-ground-projection}
\end{align}
Consequently, with \(f^\star:=\min_x f(x)\),
\begin{equation}
  0\leq f(x)-f^\star\leq F(x,y)-f^\star
  \qquad\text{for every }(x,y).
  \label{eq:app-gap-ordering}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:app-lower-envelope} follows because \(f(x)\) is the minimum
of \(F(x,\cdot)\).  Minimizing it over \(x\) gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-ground-energy}.  If \((x^\star,y^\star)\) minimizes \(F\),
then
\[
  f(x^\star)\leq F(x^\star,y^\star)
  =\min_{x,y}F(x,y)=\min_x f(x),
\]
so \(x^\star\) minimizes \(f\).  Conversely, if \(x^\star\) minimizes \(f\),
any \(y^\star\in\mathcal{Y}_F(x^\star)\) makes
\((x^\star,y^\star)\) a minimizer of \(F\).  This proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-ground-projection}; subtracting \(f^\star\) from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-lower-envelope} proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-gap-ordering}.
\end{proof}

For the diagonal encodings, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:app-ground-energy} and
\eqref{eq:app-ground-projection} give equality of the ground energies and the
correct original-variable projection of the computational-basis ground states.
They imply no equality of the excited-state spectra.

\subsection{Proof of the sharp one-step threshold}
\label{app:sharp-one-step-proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-aggregate-residual}]
Fix \(u\), write \(a=u_i\), \(b=u_j\), and \(A=A_p(v)\).  The consistent
choice \(y_p=ab\) reproduces \(abA\).  The excess at the unique inconsistent
choice is
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|c}
 (a,b) & Q_{p,M_p}(u,y_p)-q(u) \\ \hline
 (0,0) & A+3M_p \\
 (0,1)\text{ or }(1,0) & A+M_p \\
 (1,1) & M_p-A
\end{array}
\label{eq:inconsistent-excess-table}
\end{equation}
All three values are nonnegative for every \(v\) exactly when
\begin{equation}
  M_p\geq-\min_vA_p(v)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  M_p\geq\max_vA_p(v),
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to Eq.~\eqref{eq:sharp-one-step-threshold}.  Each
inconsistent excess is also at least
\(M_p-|A|\geq M_p-\|A_p\|_\infty\), proving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:one-step-margin}.  Strict inequality makes every inconsistent
choice strictly worse.  Conversely, at equality the maximum defining
\(\|A_p\|_\infty\) is attained on the finite Boolean cube, and the
corresponding second or third row of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:inconsistent-excess-table} vanishes; thus uniform fibre
uniqueness holds exactly for \(M_p>\|A_p\|_\infty\).
\end{proof}


\subsection{Proof of sequential compositional exactness}
\label{app:compositional-proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sequential-compositional-exactness}]
For an arbitrary \((x,y)\), apply
Eq.~\eqref{eq:one-step-margin} at step \(r\) to the current assignment
\(u^{(r-1)}=(x,y_{<r})\).  This gives
\begin{equation}
  g^{(r)}(x,y_{\leq r})-g^{(r-1)}(x,y_{<r})
  \geq\delta_r
  \mathbf 1_{\{y_r\neq
  a_r(x,y_{<r})b_r(x,y_{<r})\}}.
  \label{eq:stepwise-compositional-gap}
\end{equation}
Summing over \(r\) telescopes to Eq.~\eqref{eq:compositional-gap}.  For the
recursive lift \(y=\Phi(x)\), every substitution is consistent, so its
penalty vanishes and its substituted block equals the preceding block.
Hence
\begin{equation}
  g^{(R)}(x,\Phi(x))=g^{(0)}(x)=f(x),
\end{equation}
which, together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:compositional-gap}, proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:compositional-exactness}.

If every \(\delta_r>0\) and \(y\neq\Phi(x)\), let \(r_\star\) be the first
index with \(y_{r_\star}\neq\phi_{r_\star}(x)\).  Then
\(y_{<r_\star}=\phi_{<r_\star}(x)\), so the \(r_\star\)-th indicator in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:compositional-gap} equals one.  Consequently
\(g^{(R)}(x,y)-f(x)\geq\delta_{r_\star}>0\), proving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:strict-fibre-uniqueness}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Product grammar and target locality}
\label{app:product-grammar}
\label{app:target-locality-proof}

This grammar formalises standard recursive Rosenberg and monomial-decomposition
constructions~\cite{Rosenberg1975,BorosGruber2014,CramaEtAl2025}.
For completeness, identify a nonempty support \(S\subseteq[n]\) with the
product block \(x_S\).  A production
\begin{equation}
  S=B_S\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_S,
  \qquad
  y_S=v_{B_S}v_{C_S},
  \qquad
  v_T:=
  \begin{cases}
    x_i,&T=\{i\},\\
    y_T,&|T|\geq2,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:product-grammar-production}
\end{equation}
is admissible when its two smaller blocks are already available.  This
strict size order is acyclic.  A terminal partition
\(A=S_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}\cdots\mathbin{\dot\cup}S_t\) represents
\(x_A\) by \(\prod_{j=1}^t v_{S_j}\); the representation has locality
\(t\) and equals \(x_A\) on the consistent lift.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:selective-target-locality}]
Only \(A\in\mathcal D\) with \(k:=|A|>\ell\) require reduction.  Order
\(A=\{i_1,\ldots,i_k\}\) and introduce the fresh chain
\begin{equation}
  y_{A,1}=x_{i_1}x_{i_2},
  \qquad
  y_{A,s}=y_{A,s-1}x_{i_{s+1}}
  \quad(2\leq s\leq k-\ell).
  \label{eq:fresh-chain}
\end{equation}
After step \(s\), the descendant of \(c_Ax_A\) is
\begin{equation}
  c_Ay_{A,s}\prod_{t=s+2}^{k}x_{i_t}.
  \label{eq:fresh-chain-descendant}
\end{equation}
The aggregate residual of this one-term substitution is
\(c_A\prod_{t=s+2}^{k}x_{i_t}\), whose Boolean sup norm is \(|c_A|\).
Thus Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-aggregate-residual} permits
\(M_{A,s}\geq|c_A|\), and the final descendant has degree \(\ell\).

Use disjoint fresh chains for all selected supports and concatenate them in
any topological order.  Theorem~\ref{thm:sequential-compositional-exactness}
gives pointwise exactness, while the construction uses exactly
\(\sum_{A\in\mathcal D}\max\{|A|-\ell,0\}\) auxiliaries.  Strict penalties
give uniqueness by the same theorem.  If every term of degree greater than
\(\ell\) is selected, all final objective terms have degree at most
\(\ell\), since the added Rosenberg penalties are quadratic.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Auxiliary-reuse structure}
\label{app:cubic-structure}

For $\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal C_3(f)$, define the pair shadow
\(
\partial_2\mathcal D
:=
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal D}\binom{A}{2}.
\)
Within the one-pair-per-cubic-term construction, the minimum number of
auxiliaries required to reduce every term in $\mathcal D$ is the set-cover
number
\begin{equation}
\tau_2(\mathcal D)
=
\min
\left\{
\sum_{p\in\partial_2\mathcal D}z_p:
\sum_{p\in\binom{A}{2}}z_p\ge1\ \forall A\in\mathcal D,\;
z_p\in\{0,1\}
\right\}.
\label{eq:pair-cover}
\end{equation}
Related substitution-selection methods seek to minimise auxiliary variables
~\cite{VermaLewis2020,VermaLewisKochenberger2021,Schmidbauer2026};
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pair-cover} is the exact set-cover characterisation for the
present one-pair-per-cubic-term design.
Writing
\[
\Delta_2(\mathcal D)
:=
\max_{p\in\partial_2\mathcal D}
|\{A\in\mathcal D:p\subset A\}|,
\]
one has
\[
\left\lceil
\frac{|\mathcal D|}{\Delta_2(\mathcal D)}
\right\rceil
\le
\tau_2(\mathcal D)
\le
|\mathcal D|.
\]

Equivalently, pair reuse may be visualised through the overlap graph
$G_{\mathrm{ov}}$, whose vertices are cubic supports and where
$A$ and $B$ are adjacent when $|A\cap B|=2$. All terms assigned to one
auxiliary $y_p$ belong to the clique
\[
K(p):=\{A\in\mathcal C_3(f):p\subset A\}.
\]
\subsubsection{Optional linear encoding of the exact design space}
\label{app:linear-design}

For completeness, introduce
\[
r_A=\mathbf 1_{\{A\text{ retained}\}},
\qquad
s_{A,p}=\mathbf 1_{\{A\text{ reduced through }p\}},
\qquad
z_p=\mathbf 1_{\{y_p\text{ active}\}}.
\]
The exact cubic design family admits the linear description
\begin{equation}
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
r_A+\sum_{p\in\binom{A}{2}} s_{A,p} &= 1,
&& A\in\mathcal C_3(f),\\
s_{A,p} &\le z_p,
&& A\in\mathcal C_3(f),\ p\in\binom{A}{2},\\
z_p &\le
\sum_{\substack{A\in\mathcal C_3(f)\\p\subset A}} s_{A,p},
&& p\in\partial_2\mathcal C_3(f),\\
M_p &\ge
\sum_{\substack{A\supset p\\c_A>0}} c_A s_{A,p}
+\varepsilon_p z_p,
&& p\in\partial_2\mathcal C_3(f),\\
M_p &\ge
\sum_{\substack{A\supset p\\c_A<0}} |c_A| s_{A,p}
+\varepsilon_p z_p,
&& p\in\partial_2\mathcal C_3(f).
\end{aligned}
\right.
\label{eq:exact-design-system}
\end{equation}

An optional upper bound
\[
0\le M_p
\le
\left(
\sum_{A\in\mathcal C_3(f),\,p\subset A}|c_A|
+\varepsilon_p
\right)z_p
\]
forces $M_p=0$ whenever $p$ is inactive.

\section{Deterministic Compilation and Selection Details}
\label{app:certified-selection}

This appendix fixes a transparent reference architecture and compiler and
records the minimal implementation rules needed to reproduce the optional
certificate of \cref{sec:search-certification}.  Its purpose is to make the
resource objective deterministic and auditable.  It is not intended as a
specialised treatment of presolve or structural acceleration of the
representation-selection problem.



\subsection{Reference architecture and compiler}
\label{app:reference-compiler}

Fix a maximum logical width \(Q_{\max}\).  The reference architecture
\(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is the complete graph on \(Q_{\max}\) qubits
with arbitrary single-qubit \(R_z\) rotations and directed CNOT gates in
both directions.  For a design \(\sigma\) with
\(N_\sigma\leq Q_{\max}\), original variables are placed in increasing
index order, followed by active auxiliaries \(y_{\{i,j\}}\) in
lexicographic pair order.  This fixes the logical-to-physical map and gives
\(S_\sigma^{\mathrm{ref}}=0\).

Following the standard pseudo-Boolean-to-diagonal-Hamiltonian map
~\cite{Hadfield2021}, the objective is converted to collected Pauli form,
\begin{equation}
    H_{F_\sigma}
    =
    \sum_{T\subseteq[N_\sigma]}h_T^{(\sigma)}Z_T.
    \label{eq:reference-collected-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
Coefficient aggregation is performed before compilation.  Benchmark
coefficients, margins, and normalisation scales are stored exactly whenever
possible, and a coefficient is removed only when it is exactly zero.  The
QAOA parameter \(\gamma\) remains symbolic, so no gate is deleted because
of a special numerical value of \(\gamma\).

For a non-zero Pauli string \(Z_T\), write
\(T=\{q_1<\cdots<q_k\}\).  The reference compiler targets \(q_k\) and emits
the ancilla-free parity gadget
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{CX}_{q_1,q_k}\cdots
    \operatorname{CX}_{q_{k-1},q_k}
    R_z^{(q_k)}\!\left(2\gamma h_T^{(\sigma)}\right)
    \operatorname{CX}_{q_{k-1},q_k}\cdots
    \operatorname{CX}_{q_1,q_k}.
    \label{eq:reference-parity-gadget}
\end{equation}
This is the standard ancilla-free parity-gadget construction for exponentiating
a diagonal Pauli string~\cite{CampbellDahl2022,Verchere2023}.
Non-zero strings are processed first by increasing weight and then
lexicographically.  Cross-gadget cancellation, commutation-based
reordering, and approximate angle simplification are disabled.  Every
design therefore produces one fixed CNOT stream.

Define
\begin{equation}
    p_{k,\sigma}
    :=
    \left|
        \{T:|T|=k,\ h_T^{(\sigma)}\neq0\}
    \right|.
    \label{eq:reference-pauli-count}
\end{equation}
Selective cubic representations have no Pauli strings of weight greater
than three, and hence
\begin{equation}
    G_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ref}}
    =2p_{2,\sigma}+4p_{3,\sigma}.
    \label{eq:reference-gate-count}
\end{equation}

The reference two-qubit depth is obtained from a deterministic earliest-layer
schedule.  Write the emitted CNOT stream as
\((a_\ell,b_\ell)_{\ell=1}^{G_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ref}}}\), initialise
\(d_q^{(0)}=0\), and set
\begin{align}
    \lambda_\ell
    &:=
    1+\max\left\{
        d_{a_\ell}^{(\ell-1)},d_{b_\ell}^{(\ell-1)}
    \right\},
    \label{eq:reference-asap-layer}\\
    d_q^{(\ell)}
    &:=
    \begin{cases}
        \lambda_\ell,&q\in\{a_\ell,b_\ell\},\\
        d_q^{(\ell-1)},&q\notin\{a_\ell,b_\ell\}.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:reference-asap-update}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
    D_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ref}}
    :=
    \max_qd_q^{(G_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ref}})},
    \qquad
    S_\sigma^{\mathrm{ref}}:=0.
    \label{eq:reference-depth}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:reference-collected-hamiltonian}--%
\eqref{eq:reference-depth} define a finite deterministic map
\[
    \sigma\longmapsto
    \left(
        G_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ref}},
        D_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ref}},
        S_\sigma^{\mathrm{ref}}
    \right).
\]
The all-to-all connectivity separates synthesis and scheduling effects from
routing; routing sensitivity is measured with the device-level protocol
below.  Thus $D_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the post-synthesis,
post-scheduling CNOT depth on an ideal all-to-all architecture; it is neither
the polynomial locality nor a post-routing device-level depth.  Connectivity
and routing can substantially alter QAOA depth
~\cite{Weidenfeller2022,Pelofske2024,Rovara2026}.


\subsection{Deterministic device-level protocol}
\label{app:hardware-compiler-protocol}

The same selection rule applies to a hardware-specific compiler provided
that its output is made deterministic.  A reproducible protocol fixes and
reports:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item compiler package, version, and numerical tolerances;
    \item directed coupling map, basis gates, and instruction data;
    \item logical-qubit ordering and initial-layout rule;
    \item synthesis templates for two- and three-local diagonal phases;
    \item layout, routing, translation, optimisation, and scheduling passes;
    \item optimisation level and deterministic pass tie-breaking;
    \item symbolic or numerically bound parameter handling;
    \item coefficient-aggregation and zero-removal rules;
    \item a common finite transpiler-seed bundle and its aggregation
    statistic, if randomness remains; and
    \item the stage at which gate count, depth, SWAP count, and dynamic
    range are extracted.
\end{enumerate}
Fixing these compiler choices is necessary because reduction, synthesis,
routing, and transpiler settings can otherwise change the reported circuit
resources~\cite{Schmidbauer2024,Weidenfeller2022,Rovara2026}.
If a seed bundle \(\mathcal S\) is used, every candidate is evaluated with
the same complete bundle.  The resulting compiler oracle
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal O_{\mathcal A,\mathcal C}(\sigma)
    :=
    \left(
        G_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathcal A,\mathcal C},
        D_{2q,\sigma}^{\mathcal A,\mathcal C},
        S_\sigma^{\mathcal A,\mathcal C}
    \right)
    \label{eq:deterministic-compiler-oracle}
\end{equation}
is deterministic.  Any global optimality statement is relative to this
declared architecture and compiler protocol, rather than to all possible
compilers for the device.


\subsection{Relaxation-derived fibre diagnostics}
\label{app:fibre-risk}

Let
\(
X_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\mu_i),
\,
Y_p\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_p)
\)
independently, and write $t_p:=\mu_i\mu_j$ for $p=\{i,j\}$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\nu_p
=
\Pr(Y_p\neq X_iX_j)
=
q_p(1-t_p)+(1-q_p)t_p.
\label{eq:fibre-violation-probability}
\end{equation}
and
\[
\ell_p
=
\mathbb E[P_{\mathrm R}(X_i,X_j,Y_p)]
=
t_p+q_p(3-2\mu_i-2\mu_j).
\]
For a design $\sigma$, define
\[
A_p(\mu)
:=
\sum_{A\in\mathcal D_p} c_A
\prod_{k\in A\setminus p}\mu_k.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma;\mu,q)
=
\sum_{p\in P_\rho}
\left[
(q_p-t_p)A_p(\mu)+M_p\ell_p
\right].
\label{eq:fibre-excess-full}
\end{equation}
When $q_p=t_p$, the interaction correction vanishes.
For design-independent normalisation one may fix
\[
s_{\mathrm{fib}}
=
4\sum_{p\in\partial_2\mathcal C_3(f)}
\left(
\sum_{A\in\mathcal C_3(f),\,p\subset A}|c_A|
+\varepsilon_p
\right).
\]
Additional reported diagnostics include
\[
\Lambda_\sigma
:=
\min\left\{1,\sum_{p\in P_\rho}\nu_p\right\},
\]
and, for $P_\rho\neq\varnothing$,
\[
R_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma)
:=
\frac{E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma)}
{4\sum_{p\in P_\rho}M_p}.
\]
These quantities are diagnostics rather than independent correctness
conditions.

\subsection{Deterministic selector subroutines}
\label{app:selector-details}

For the ordered cubic supports $(A_1,\ldots,A_m)$, define the action set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal U_t:=\{\bot\}\cup\binom{A_t}{2},
\label{eq:app-action-set}
\end{equation}
where $\bot$ retains $A_t$ natively and $p\in\binom{A_t}{2}$ reduces $A_t$ through
pair $p$.  A depth-$k$ state is $s=(k,\alpha_{1:k})$ with
$\alpha_t\in\mathcal U_t$.  Its native completion and children are
\begin{equation}
\bar\alpha(s):=(\alpha_{1:k},\bot^{m-k}),
\qquad
\bar\sigma(s):=\sigma(\bar\alpha(s)),
\qquad
\operatorname{child}(s,u):=(k+1,\alpha_{1:k},u).
\label{eq:app-state-completion}
\end{equation}

For a partial state, let
\begin{align}
P_s&:=\{p:\alpha_t=p\text{ for some }t\le k\},
&a_s&:=|P_s|,\label{eq:app-partial-pairs}\\
C^+_{p,s}&:=\sum_{\substack{t\le k:\,\alpha_t=p\\ c_{A_t}>0}}c_{A_t},
&C^-_{p,s}&:=\sum_{\substack{t\le k:\,\alpha_t=p\\ c_{A_t}<0}}|c_{A_t}|,
\label{eq:app-signed-masses}\\
M_{p,s}&:=\max\{C^+_{p,s},C^-_{p,s}\}+\varepsilon_p,
&M_{\max,s}&:=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{M_{p,s}:p\in P_s\}\bigr).
\label{eq:app-partial-penalties}
\end{align}
Let $G_{\mathrm{lb}}(s)$ and $D_{\mathrm{lb}}(s)$ be verified lower bounds on the
compiled two-qubit gate count and depth of every leaf below $s$; the default
$G_{\mathrm{lb}}(s)=D_{\mathrm{lb}}(s)=0$ is always valid.  The certified scalar bound is
\begin{equation}
L_J(s):=
w_a\frac{a_s}{s_a}
+w_G\frac{G_{\mathrm{lb}}(s)}{s_G}
+w_D\frac{D_{\mathrm{lb}}(s)}{s_D}
+w_M\frac{M_{\max,s}}{s_M}.
\label{eq:app-certified-node-bound}
\end{equation}
The compiled metrics of $\bar\sigma(s)$ may be used to order the search but are not
certified subtree bounds unless this has been proved separately.

For the empirical Pareto archive, use the normalised vector
\begin{equation}
    r_{\mathrm{sel}}(\sigma)
    :=
    \left(
        \frac{a_\sigma}{s_a},
        \frac{G_{2q,\sigma}}{s_G},
        \frac{D_{2q,\sigma}}{s_D},
        \frac{M_{\max,\sigma}}{s_M},
        \widehat E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma)
    \right).
    \label{eq:app-pareto-vector}
\end{equation}

All subroutines share the mutable state
\begin{equation}
\mathsf X
:=(\mathcal K,\mathcal V,\mathcal P_{\mathrm{emp}},
\sigma_{\mathrm{inc}},U_J),
\label{eq:app-shared-search-state}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal K$ is the deterministic cache and $\mathcal V$ contains only evaluated
complete designs.  Partial states are never inserted into $\mathcal V$.  Incumbent ties
are resolved by the total order
\begin{equation}
\sigma\prec_{\mathrm{inc}}\tau
\iff
\bigl(J_{\mathrm{res}}(\sigma),\widehat E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\sigma),\alpha(\sigma)\bigr)
<_{\mathrm{lex}}
\bigl(J_{\mathrm{res}}(\tau),\widehat E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\tau),\alpha(\tau)\bigr).
\label{eq:app-incumbent-order}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Candidate evaluation and global-state update}
\label{alg:app-evaluate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Complete design $\sigma=\sigma(\alpha_{1:m})$; state $\mathsf X$;
fixed protocol $\Pi=(\mathcal A,\mathcal C,\mathcal S,\operatorname{Agg})$.
\Ensure Exactly one deterministic cache record for $\sigma$ and an updated state
$\mathsf X$ satisfying Equation~\eqref{eq:app-shared-search-state}.
\State $q\gets\operatorname{key}(\sigma)
:=(\alpha_{1:m},\mathcal A,\mathcal C,\mathcal S)$
\If{$q\notin\mathcal K$}
    \State Construct the canonical $F_\sigma$; compute algebraic resources,
    fibre diagnostics, and pre-compilation feasibility flags.
    \If{$\sigma$ violates a pre-compilation hard constraint}
        \State Set compiler-dependent metrics and $J_{\mathrm{res}}(\sigma)$ to $+\infty$;
        set $\mathrm{feas}(\sigma)\gets\mathrm{false}$.
    \Else
        \State Compile $F_\sigma$ for every seed in the complete common bundle
        $\mathcal S$; if $\mathcal S=\varnothing$, compile once without a seed.
        \State Aggregate each compiler metric with $\operatorname{Agg}$; compute
        $J_{\mathrm{res}}(\sigma)$, $r_{\mathrm{sel}}(\sigma)$, and
        $\mathrm{feas}(\sigma):=\mathbf1[\sigma\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}]$.
    \EndIf
    \State Store the complete record as $\mathcal K[q]$.
\EndIf
\State Load $\mathcal K[q]$ and set $\mathcal V\gets\mathcal V\cup\{\sigma\}$.
\State $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{emp}}\gets
\operatorname{ND}_{r_{\mathrm{sel}}}
(\mathcal V\cap\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}})$
\If{$\sigma\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}$ and
$(\sigma_{\mathrm{inc}}=\textsc{None}$ or
$\sigma\prec_{\mathrm{inc}}\sigma_{\mathrm{inc}})$}
    \State $\sigma_{\mathrm{inc}}\gets\sigma$;
    $U_J\gets J_{\mathrm{res}}(\sigma)$
\EndIf
\State \Return $\mathsf X$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For beam selection, write $s\prec_J t$ when
\begin{equation}
\bigl(
\mathbf1[\bar\sigma(s)\notin\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}],
J_{\mathrm{res}}(\bar\sigma(s)),
\widehat E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\bar\sigma(s)),
\bar\alpha(s)
\bigr)
<_{\mathrm{lex}}
\bigl(
\mathbf1[\bar\sigma(t)\notin\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}],
J_{\mathrm{res}}(\bar\sigma(t)),
\widehat E_{\mathrm{fib}}(\bar\sigma(t)),
\bar\alpha(t)
\bigr).
\label{eq:app-beam-score-order}
\end{equation}
Within a fixed candidate set $\mathcal C$, the Pareto order $\prec_P$ sorts states by
\begin{equation}
\bigl(
\mathbf1[\bar\sigma(s)\notin\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}],
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal C}(s),
-\operatorname{crowd}_{\mathcal C}(s),
\bar\alpha(s)
\bigr)
\label{eq:app-beam-pareto-order}
\end{equation}
in increasing lexicographic order, where rank and crowding distance are computed from
$r_{\mathrm{sel}}(\bar\sigma(s))$ using the standard NSGA-II convention
~\cite{DebEtAl2002}.  These total orders affect search order only and do not
justify certified pruning.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Deterministic width-$B$ Pareto-beam search}
\label{alg:app-pareto-beam}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Root $s_0$, width $B\ge1$, state $\mathsf X$, protocol $\Pi$, and stopping
rule $\operatorname{Stop}_{\mathrm{beam}}$.
\Ensure Updated state $\mathsf X$; discarded beam states carry no certification claim.
\State $\mathcal B_0\gets\{s_0\}$
\For{$k=0,\ldots,m-1$}
    \State $\mathcal C_{k+1}\gets\varnothing$
    \ForAll{$s\in\mathcal B_k$ in lexicographic prefix order}
        \ForAll{$u\in\mathcal U_{k+1}$ in the fixed action order}
            \State $s'\gets\operatorname{child}(s,u)$;
            $\mathcal C_{k+1}\gets\mathcal C_{k+1}\cup\{s'\}$
            \If{$\operatorname{key}(\bar\sigma(s'))\notin\mathcal K$ and
            $\operatorname{Stop}_{\mathrm{beam}}(\mathsf X)=\mathrm{true}$}
                \State \Return $\mathsf X$
            \EndIf
            \State $\mathsf X\gets
            \Call{EvaluateCandidate}{\bar\sigma(s'),\mathsf X,\Pi}$
            \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:app-evaluate}}
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \State $b\gets\min\{B,|\mathcal C_{k+1}|\}$;
    $b_J\gets\lceil b/2\rceil$
    \State $\mathcal S_J\gets$ the first $b_J$ states of $\mathcal C_{k+1}$ under
    $\prec_J$.
    \State $\mathcal S_P\gets$ the first $b-b_J$ states of
    $\mathcal C_{k+1}\setminus\mathcal S_J$ under $\prec_P$.
    \State $\mathcal B_{k+1}\gets\mathcal S_J\cup\mathcal S_P$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathsf X$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Certification maintains the frontier invariant
\begin{quote}
Every leaf not yet evaluated and not removed by a valid infeasibility or lower-bound
proof lies below at least one state in $\mathrm{Open}$.
\end{quote}
Thus $\mathrm{Open}$ contains unresolved partial or complete states; membership
$s\in\mathrm{Open}$ does not imply $\bar\sigma(s)\in\mathcal V$.  Define
$\operatorname{CertInfeasible}(s)$ to be true only when every leaf below $s$ lies outside
$\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sel}}$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Anytime branch-and-bound certification}
\label{alg:app-branch-and-bound}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Root $s_0$, state $\mathsf X$, protocol $\Pi$, and deterministic stopping rule
$\operatorname{Stop}_{\mathrm{cert}}$.
\Require $L_J(s)$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:app-certified-node-bound} and certified
predicate $\operatorname{CertInfeasible}(s)$.
\Ensure Complete unresolved frontier $\mathrm{Open}$, updated state $\mathsf X$,
status, and $L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}$ satisfying \cref{prop:anytime-certificate}.
\State $\mathrm{Open}\gets\{s_0\}$
\While{$\mathrm{Open}\ne\varnothing$ and
$\operatorname{Stop}_{\mathrm{cert}}(\mathsf X,\mathrm{Open})=\mathrm{false}$}
    \State Choose $s^\star$ minimising
    $(L_J(s),\alpha_{1:k}(s))$ lexicographically over $s\in\mathrm{Open}$.
    \If{$\operatorname{CertInfeasible}(s^\star)=\mathrm{true}$ or
    $L_J(s^\star)>U_J$}
        \State $\mathrm{Open}\gets\mathrm{Open}\setminus\{s^\star\}$
    \ElsIf{$s^\star=(m,\alpha_{1:m})$}
        \If{$\operatorname{key}(\sigma(\alpha_{1:m}))\notin\mathcal K$ and the remaining
        budget cannot complete the full seed bundle $\mathcal S$}
            \State \textbf{break}
        \EndIf
        \State $\mathsf X\gets
        \Call{EvaluateCandidate}{\sigma(\alpha_{1:m}),\mathsf X,\Pi}$
        \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:app-evaluate}}
        \State $\mathrm{Open}\gets\mathrm{Open}\setminus\{s^\star\}$
    \Else
        \State $\mathcal C\gets
        \{\operatorname{child}(s^\star,u):u\in\mathcal U_{k+1}\}$
        \State $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{keep}}\gets
        \{s\in\mathcal C:
        \operatorname{CertInfeasible}(s)=\mathrm{false},\ L_J(s)\le U_J\}$
        \State Atomically set
        $\mathrm{Open}\gets
        (\mathrm{Open}\setminus\{s^\star\})\cup\mathcal C_{\mathrm{keep}}$.
    \EndIf
\EndWhile
\State $L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}\gets
\min\bigl(\{U_J\}\cup\{L_J(s):s\in\mathrm{Open}\}\bigr)$
\If{$U_J=+\infty$ and $\mathrm{Open}=\varnothing$}
    \State $\mathrm{status}\gets\textsc{Infeasible}$
\ElsIf{$U_J<+\infty$ and
$\nexists s\in\mathrm{Open}:L_J(s)\le U_J$}
    \State $\mathrm{status}\gets\textsc{Optimum-And-Tie-Break-Certified}$
\ElsIf{$U_J<+\infty$ and $L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}=U_J$}
    \State $\mathrm{status}\gets\textsc{Optimal-Value-Certified}$
\Else
    \State $\mathrm{status}\gets$ the declared stopping-rule status.
\EndIf
\State \Return $(\mathsf X,\mathrm{Open},L_J^{\mathrm{glob}},\mathrm{status})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The cache key is the complete action vector together with
$(\mathcal A,\mathcal C,\mathcal S)$; undecided coordinates of a partial state are filled
with $\bot$ before lookup.  A seed-bundle evaluation begins only if the entire bundle can
be completed.  Frontier replacement in Algorithm~\ref{alg:app-branch-and-bound} is
atomic: its parent remains open until every child has either been inserted or removed by
a certified test.  Valid stopping statuses are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item budget exhausted;
    \item additive target reached,
    $U_J-L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}$;
    \item multiplicative target reached, when $L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}>0$,
    $U_J/L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}\le1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{mult}}$;
    \item optimal value or optimal value with deterministic tie-break certified.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cache, frontier, and stopping rules}
\label{app:selection-implementation}
\label{app:selection-stopping-rules}

The cache key is
\begin{equation}
    \mathsf{key}(\sigma)
    :=
    \bigl(
        \alpha_{1:m},
        \mathcal A,
        \mathcal C,
        \mathcal S
    \bigr),
    \label{eq:appendix-cache-key}
\end{equation}
where \(\alpha_{1:m}\) is the complete termwise action vector and
\(\mathcal S\) is empty when the compiler has no random seed.  For a
partial-state representative, undecided coordinates are filled by the
native action \(\bot\); the partial prefix itself is not used as the key.
The stored record contains the canonical penalties, compiled metrics,
dynamic range, fibre-risk diagnostics, and feasibility flags.

The beam policy is fixed before the experiment.  A reproducible default
reserves half of its width for the smallest resource scores and fills the
remaining slots by non-dominated resource rank and crowding distance;
lexicographic action-vector order resolves final ties.  This ordering affects
which candidates are found early but does not provide a certificate.

Certification requires the frontier invariant
\begin{quote}
Every leaf not yet evaluated and not removed by a valid infeasibility or
bound proof lies below at least one state in \(\mathsf{Open}\).
\end{quote}
Thus states omitted from the current batch remain open, and beam truncation
is never treated as certified pruning.  The always-valid compiler lower
bounds are
\(\underline G(s)=\underline D(s)=0\).  Stronger bounds may be substituted
only after proving that they apply to every completion below the node.  In
particular, compiled metrics of the current native completion and learned
compiler surrogates may order states but may not certify pruning.

Frontier updates are atomic at each reported checkpoint: a parent remains
open until all of its actions have been processed or recorded as unresolved.
If a seed bundle is used, a candidate evaluation begins only when the
remaining budget can complete that bundle.  These rules ensure that a
wall-time interruption does not invalidate
\cref{prop:anytime-certificate}.

Let \(U_t\) be the incumbent value after \(t\) completed evaluations and let
\(L_t\) be the global lower bound defined by \cref{eq:global-lower-bound}.  Every
certification run reports one of three statuses:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{budget exhausted}: return the incumbent and the additive
    interval \(L_t\leq J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star\leq U_t\);
    \item \emph{target gap reached}: stop once
    \(U_t-L_t\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}\), or, when \(L_t>0\), once
    \(U_t/L_t\leq1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{mult}}\);
    \item \emph{global optimum certified}: report
    \(U_t=L_t=J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star\).
\end{enumerate}
The quantity \((U_t-L_t)/U_t\) is an incumbent-normalised gap, not a
multiplicative approximation ratio to the unknown optimum.  A rigorous
multiplicative factor is \(U_t/L_t\), which requires \(L_t>0\).  When
\(L_t=0<U_t\), only the additive certificate is available.


\subsection{Proof of the anytime selection certificate}
\label{app:anytime-certificate-proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:anytime-certificate}]
Every evaluated candidate belongs to $\Sigma_{\mathrm{ex}}^\varepsilon$ by
construction, so pointwise exactness follows from
\cref{prop:cubic-mixed-sign}.  Since $U_J$ is the objective value of an
evaluated feasible design, $J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star\le U_J$ whenever
$U_J<+\infty$.

Let $\sigma^\star$ be an optimal feasible design.  If $\sigma^\star$ has
already been evaluated, then $U_J=J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star$ and hence
$L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}\le J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star$.  Otherwise, the frontier
invariant places $\sigma^\star$ below some unresolved state
$s\in\mathrm{Open}$.  Admissibility of \cref{eq:admissible-bound} gives
$L_J(s)\le J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star$, and therefore
$L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}\le J_{\mathrm{res}}^\star$ by
\cref{eq:global-lower-bound}.  Combining the two inequalities proves
\cref{eq:anytime-certificate}; the additive-gap statement and the
optimality criterion $L_J^{\mathrm{glob}}=U_J$ follow immediately.
\end{proof}

\section{Warm-Start and Cost-Layer Circuit Details}
\label{app:warm-start-circuits}

For the matched single-qubit warm-start mixer
~\cite{Egger2021,TateCustom2023}, let
\[
\theta_\omega:=2\arcsin\sqrt{\omega},
\]
so that
\[
e^{-i\beta h_M(\omega)}
=
R_y(\theta_\omega)
R_z(-2\beta)
R_y(-\theta_\omega).
\]
Hence the complete mixer factorises into parallel single-qubit gates,
\[
U_{M,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ws}}(\beta)
=
\prod_{i=1}^n U_M^{(\mu_i)}(\beta)
\prod_{p\in P_\rho}U_M^{(q_p)}(\beta).
\]

For the cost layer,
\[
F_\sigma(x,y)
=
f_{\le2}(x)
+\sum_{A\in\mathcal R}c_Ax_A
+\sum_{p\in P_\rho}y_pA_p(x)
+\sum_{p=\{i,j\}\in P_\rho}
M_pP_{\mathrm R}(x_i,x_j,y_p).
\]
After $u_j\mapsto N_j=(I-Z_j)/2$, all resulting operators are diagonal and
commute~\cite{Hadfield2021,CampbellDahl2022}.  In particular,
\[
e^{-i\gamma M_p P_{\mathrm R}(N_i,N_j,N_{y_p})}
=
e^{-i\gamma M_pN_iN_j}
e^{2i\gamma M_pN_iN_{y_p}}
e^{2i\gamma M_pN_jN_{y_p}}
e^{-3i\gamma M_pN_{y_p}}.
\]
Thus quadratizing a cubic term replaces its three-local logical phase by an
auxiliary qubit together with one- and two-local penalty phases
~\cite{Schmidbauer2024,Schmidbauer2026}.  All
representations are subsequently transpiled using the common protocol of
Appendix~\ref{app:hardware-compiler-protocol}.



\subsection{Alternative auxiliary initialisations}
\label{app:alternative-warm-starts}

Two comparison initialisations may be used in ablation studies.

A rounded construction sets
\[
\hat x_i=\mathbf 1\{\mu_i\ge 1/2\},
\qquad
\hat y_p=\hat x_i\hat x_j,
\]
giving an exactly consistent computational-basis state.

Alternatively, auxiliary products may be computed coherently after preparing
the original warm-start superposition~\cite{Egger2021}:
\[
U_\rho
|x\rangle|0\rangle^{\otimes |P_\rho|}
=
|x\rangle|\phi_\rho(x)\rangle .
\]
This removes initial auxiliary inconsistencies but requires additional
entangling gates and does not by itself make a subsequent local mixer
constraint preserving.




\section{Max-3SAT Formulation Details}
\label{app:max3sat}
% =============================================================================

Using the standard pseudo-Boolean clause-violation encoding
~\cite{Nagies2025,Nusslein2023}, for a clause with signs
$\sigma_r\in\{+,-\}$ define
\begin{equation}
    u_r(x_r)=\begin{cases}1-x_r,&\sigma_r=+,\\x_r,&\sigma_r=-.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Then $P_C=u_iu_ju_k$. Expanding gives a constant, linear, quadratic, and cubic part. For example,
\begin{align}
    (x_i\lor x_j\lor x_k):\quad
    P_C&=(1-x_i)(1-x_j)(1-x_k),\\
    (\neg x_i\lor x_j\lor x_k):\quad
    P_C&=x_i(1-x_j)(1-x_k),\\
    (\neg x_i\lor\neg x_j\lor x_k):\quad
    P_C&=x_ix_j(1-x_k),\\
    (\neg x_i\lor\neg x_j\lor\neg x_k):\quad
    P_C&=x_ix_jx_k.
\end{align}
The remaining patterns follow by permuting variables. Coefficients from all clauses must be aggregated before the cubic support, pair shadow, or exact penalties are computed, because cancellation can remove monomials or reduce the signed masses associated with a shared pair.

\section{Cubic Spin-Glass Formulation Details}
\label{app:spin-glass}
% =============================================================================

For the higher-order spin-glass benchmark family
~\cite{Basso2022,PelofskeShortDepth2024,Pelofske2024}, set $s_i=1-2x_i$,
\begin{align}
    s_is_j&=1-2x_i-2x_j+4x_ix_j,\\
    s_is_js_k&=1-2(x_i+x_j+x_k)
      +4(x_ix_j+x_ix_k+x_jx_k)-8x_ix_jx_k.
\end{align}
Substitution into \cref{eq:cubic-spin} and coefficient aggregation produces the
binary objective.  In the main benchmark $c=0$, $h_i=0$, and $J_{ij}=0$.
Unordered cubic hyperedges are sampled without replacement, with independent
Rademacher coefficients $K_{ijk}\in\{-1,+1\}$.  Target hyperedge loads are
$m_{\mathrm e}\simeq n$, $2n$, and $4n$.  Uniform hyperedge proposals and
anchor-pair proposals provide candidate pools with different realised pair
reuse, while the coefficient law remains unchanged.  The raw mathematical
objective is not normalised as a function of $n$ or $m_{\mathrm e}$; any later
positive circuit rescaling is stored explicitly and is common to every
representation of the same instance.
